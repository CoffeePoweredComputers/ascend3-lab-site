Turn everyone's own codes into one codebook two people would apply the same way. Wiki: [the codebook and team coding](/wiki/qualitative/codebook-and-team-coding).

## The merge

1. The lead runs the merge, once. It groups codes that mean the same thing and proposes a name and definition for each group.
2. Meet, with one screen shared. Go through the proposed codes.
3. For each personal code, choose where it goes: into a proposed code, a code of its own, or dropped. Moving codes out of a group is how you split it.
4. Rewrite names and definitions until everyone would apply them the same way.
5. When nothing is undecided, write the result to the codebook draft.

## Reading a proposed code

- **Why together** gives the reason and how many cards the codes share.
- "No shared cards" means they were grouped on wording alone. Check those first.
- Open a personal code's cards and its owner's jots to see what they meant.
- The same name with different definitions is two codes.

## The draft

The merge makes one question, **Codes**, where any number can apply to a response. In the draft you can add include and exclude rules, move codes into a second question, or change a question to single choice.

Aim for six to ten topic areas. If a response often takes three or more, two codes overlap: write "exclude when" for one of them.

Anyone can edit the draft and publish it. Publish with a note saying what the team decided.

## Later

A second question could record which lens a response reflects. The student's own answer stays hidden and comes out in the export for comparison.

## Versions

- A published version never changes. To change anything, start the next draft and publish it with a note.
- Keep a code's key the same across versions. Use a new key if its meaning changes.
