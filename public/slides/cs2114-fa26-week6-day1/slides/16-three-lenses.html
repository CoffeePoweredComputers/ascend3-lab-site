THREE LENSES

Three ways to say why something is wrong

Three cards are dealt in one at a time, each with a line drawing and the one question that lens asks. Utilitarianism, orange: a balance with a crowd of small figures on one pan; which choice does the most good for the most people. Virtue ethics, in the maroon-orange mix: a figure facing its own reflection; what would an honest engineer do, and what kind of person does this make me. Kantian ethics, blue: a tablet of rules with a check mark; what if everyone did it, and am I using someone without their say. The next three slides take one lens each.

Thirty seconds: three lenses, one question each. Step 1: utilitarianism asks which choice does the most good for the most people. Step 2: virtue ethics asks what an honest engineer would do, and what kind of person doing this makes me. Step 3: Kantian ethics asks what if everyone did it, and whether I am using someone without their say; the outcome is not what decides it, which is the point of having a third lens.
