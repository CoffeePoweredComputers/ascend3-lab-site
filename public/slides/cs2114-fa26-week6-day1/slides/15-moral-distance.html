One more term, after the three lenses: why they are hard to apply. Step 1: every lens asks about the people your code affects — who ends up worse off, what building it makes you, whether anyone is being used. Step 2: you never meet those people. One engineer writes one commit, and it affects people far away who were not there when it was written. Step 3: that is moral distance — the farther you are from the people your code affects, the less it feels like your decision. It is not a character flaw; it is how being far away works, and software puts you far away by default. Step 4: three ways it shows up on a team. "Not my department" is the Lehrer line from Week 1, von Braun's rockets. "Many hands": nobody made the whole decision, so nobody feels they made any of it. "A number, not a person": the people are a metric on a dashboard until you picture one of them. So when a lens tells you something is wrong and it still does not feel like anything, the distance is why. Next: ten minutes on a concern of your own.
