Lens 3, duty: Kant. Duty means doing what is right because it is right, not for what you get out of it; the outcome is not what makes an act right or wrong. Step 1: the words. Kant gives two principles, and the slide names them. Step 2, the Formula of Universal Law: the slide asks "what if everyone did it?" Be careful how you read it: not "how much harm would that do", which is adding up outcomes again, but "could the practice survive". It is a consistency test: some acts only work as an exception. A lie works because people generally tell the truth; if lying were the rule, nobody would believe anyone and the lie would not work. Skipping the tests works because everyone else runs theirs. The false promise is Kant's own example: it works only because people keep promises, and if everyone broke them nobody would believe one, so the rule defeats itself. Step 3, the Formula of Humanity: never treat a person merely as a means. You use a taxi driver to get across town, and that is fine because they agreed to it; the fare is the consent. The users of a feature are the taxi driver. These two are the questions to use in the discussion. Step 4: the weakness — there are no exceptions, even when the result is terrible. Kant held that you may not lie even to a murderer at the door asking where your friend is. Most people think that is wrong, and it is the standard objection. Then say it: the same thing can be wrong for three different reasons, and you can hold more than one at once. Step 5: the two questions for technology, one per principle: if every app did this, would anyone still trust an app? And did your users agree to what you do with their data?
