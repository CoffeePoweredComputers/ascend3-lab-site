How many compares and how many moves does it take to sort five entries?

Day 1 was the sorted list. Step 1. This is a sorted array of eight entries. A sorted contains can stop early: contains(9) compares 3, 5 and 8, which are smaller than 9, and then 12. 12 is larger than 9, so 9 cannot be anywhere after it, and the search stops after four compares instead of eight. That only works because the entries are in order. Step 2. Today’s array is 64, 25, 12, 22 and 11, in no order. Sorting is the step that puts an array in order, so that a search like Day 1’s can rely on it. Both sorts today run on these five entries. Step 3. Ask the room for a guess: how many compares, and how many moves, does it take to sort five entries? Part 1 counts them for insertion sort.
