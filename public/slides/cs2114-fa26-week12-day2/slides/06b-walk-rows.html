Answer: A, insertion sort [twin sorting/u12q03_sorting_tracing_c; confirm in Runestone’s results view]. Step 1: from row 1 to row 2, only 25 and 64 changed places. That is insertInOrder(data, 1), slide 04. Step 2: 12 went to the front and 25 and 64 moved right one slot each, slide 04b’s second call. Step 3: 22 went into index 1, between 12 and 25. Step 4: 11 went to the front. Each row has one more entry in the sorted part on the left, and the entries on the right are still in their original order: 22 and 11 do not move until their own pass. Step 5: selection sort. Its first pass swaps the smallest entry, 11, into slot 0, so its second row starts with 11, and 64 is at the end. Step 6: bubble sort compares neighbors left to right and swaps them when they are out of order, so its first pass carries 64 to the end. Step 7: merge sort, splitting at the middle index, sorts 64 25 12 first, and its first two rows happen to match; its next step works on 22 and 11, which is not row 4. Step 8: A. In insertion sort the right part of the array is untouched until its pass; in selection sort the left part is final after each pass. That is the difference to remember.
