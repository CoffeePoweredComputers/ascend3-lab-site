Answer: B and D [keys from the PrairieLearn twin sorting/u12q06_b; confirm in Runestone’s results view]. The array is the one slide 08 left, at the start of the pass for lh = 1. Step 1: B. findSmallest compares each entry of the unsorted part with the smallest found so far: 12 with 25, then 22 with 12, then 64 with 12. That is how it determines the next sorted item. Step 2: D. One swap puts the pick, 12, into slot lh. It never moves again, so that is its final destination. Step 3: A. Selection sort never compares an unsorted entry with the sorted part; 11 is not looked at again. Comparing an unsorted item with the sorted items is what insertion sort does. Step 4: C. Comparing with the neighbor, one step at a time, until the destination is found is insertInOrder’s while loop. Step 5: E. Moving sorted items up to open the insertion point is data[j + 1] = data[j], the shift from slide 04. C and E describe insertion sort; that is the distinction both votes test. Step 6: B and D.
