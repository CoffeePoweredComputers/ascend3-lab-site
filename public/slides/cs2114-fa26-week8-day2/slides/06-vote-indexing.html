A peer-instruction question. Under the title, the question stem in muted type: suppose we have a Queue implemented with a circular array with one unused slot. The capacity of the queue is 10 and the size is 5. Which are legal values for front and rear? Below it, two panels side by side. Left, a blank array of eleven slots numbered 0 to 10. Right, the five options as rows, each led by a maroon disc with its letter: A, front 0, rear 5; B, front 4, rear 9; C, front 7, rear 2; D, front 9, rear 2; E, all of the above. Two clocks sit in the top right corner: a ninety-second vote timer and a two-and-a-half-minute discussion timer. The first press starts the vote timer; the second starts the discussion timer and ends the vote timer. Nothing else builds.

Suppose we have a Queue implemented with a circular array with one unused slot. The capacity of the queue is 10 and the size is 5.
Which are legal values for front and rear?

  1. 0
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. 3
  5. 4
  6. 5
  7. 6
  8. 7
  9. 8
  10. 9
  11. 10
  1. A

    front: 0 rear: 5

  3. C

  5. E

Answer: D — front 9, rear 2 [key from the PrairieLearn twin u10q08_circulararrayqueue_b, which is this question verbatim; confirm in Runestone’s results view]. The question asks which front and rear values are legal for a queue on a circular array with one unused slot, capacity 10 and size 5. The first press starts the 1:30 vote clock; students answer on their own first. The second press starts the 2:30 discussion clock, and the third moves on. If a group has no way in, tell them that front and rear are frontIndex and backIndex from Part 1, and ask them to count the occupied slots on the drawing for each option.
