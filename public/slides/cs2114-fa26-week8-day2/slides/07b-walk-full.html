Answer: E — five elements in the queue [key from the PrairieLearn twin; confirm in Runestone’s results view]. Step 1: capacity 5 with one unused entry is an array of six slots, 0 to 5. frontIndex is 3 and backIndex is 1. backIndex is less than frontIndex, so the queue goes past slot 5 to slot 0. Steps 2 to 6: count from the front, one slot per press: 3, 4, 5, then (5 + 1) % 6 is 0, then 1, which is the back. That is five entries. Slot 2 is the remaining slot, the unused one. Step 7: the full test from Part 1 gives the same result. backIndex plus 2 is 3, and 3 mod 6 is 3, which equals frontIndex. Step 8: the wrong answers. Four comes from stopping at slot 4, or from counting 3 to 1 as if there were no slot 5. Six would need every slot, and this design always leaves one slot empty. Invalid is wrong because both indices are legal and a full queue is a legal state. Empty, one and three do not match the count. Step 9: the verdict. Runestone activity vtcs2_circularArray_full.
