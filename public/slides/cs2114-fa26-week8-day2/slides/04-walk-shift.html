A QUEUE ON AN ARRAY · FIRST TRY

Keep the front at slot 0, and dequeue has to shift

A walkthrough of a first-try dequeue for a queue kept in an array with its front always at slot 0, under a program-counter bar: read contents at 0 into front, then a loop that copies contents at i plus 1 into contents at i, then n comes down, the old last slot is cleared, and front is returned. On the right, the call, the values of n and front, and a large counter labelled moves. Under both, a ten-slot array holding A, B, C, D and E in slots 0 to 4. Step one: slot 0 takes a blue edge, front becomes A, the counter reads 1. Steps two to five: the bar stays on the loop while B, C, D and E each slide one slot to the left, the value they land on fading as they arrive, and the counter rolls to 5. Step six: n is struck from 5 to 4. Step seven: the E left in slot 4 fades. Step eight: the caption says five entries, five moves: removing one entry costs n moves.

dequeue, first try — the front stays at slot 0

  1. T dequeue() {
  2. T front = contents[0];
  3. for (int i = 0; i < n - 1; i++)
  4. contents[i] = contents[i + 1];
  5. n--; contents[n] = null;
  6. return front;
  7. }

Five entries, five moves: one read, four shifts. Removing one entry costs n moves.

This is the first design anyone tries: keep the front of the queue at slot 0, the way a line of people shuffles forward. It is not the module’s code; contents is the array and n is the number of entries. We count moves of entries: reads and copies. Step 1: read slot 0, that is the front, A; one move. Steps 2 to 5: the loop, one press per entry. B, C, D and E each move up one slot; the counter goes to 5. Step 6: n comes down. Step 7: the old last slot is cleared, because E now sits in slot 3 as well. Step 8: five entries, five moves, and the n minus one copies are the part that grows. A thousand entries in the queue, a thousand moves to take one out. Ask: the entries already sit in order; what if they stayed where they are? Next: the module’s answer, which moves the front instead.
