On a chain, dequeue moved firstNode. What does dequeue do to the array?

Monday’s class (Tuesday’s for the TR section) put a queue on a linked chain. Today the same queue goes in an array. Step 1. This is Day 1’s linked queue after enqueue of A, B and C. firstNode refers to the front node and lastNode to the back node. The column in the middle is the queue’s operations: enqueue, dequeue, getFront, isEmpty and clear. Step 2. The class keeps the same five operations and stores the entries in an array instead. In place of firstNode and lastNode it keeps two indices, frontIndex for the slot of the front entry and backIndex for the slot of the back entry. A, B and C are in slots 0 to 2. Step 3. This is the third time we store one ADT two ways. The bag had ArrayBag and LinkedBag in Weeks 3 and 4, and the stack had ArrayStack and LinkedStack in Week 5. Each time the interface stayed the same and the cost of some methods changed. Step 4. On the chain, dequeue moved firstNode to the next node and nothing else moved. Ask the class what dequeue should do to the array. Part 1 starts with the obvious design and counts its moves.
