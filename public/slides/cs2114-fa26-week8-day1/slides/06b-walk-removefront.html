removeFront(): the new front node forgets the old one

A walkthrough of removeFront from the module's video under a program-counter bar: T removeFront; T front equals getFront; firstNode equals firstNode dot getNext; if firstNode is null, lastNode equals null; else firstNode dot setPrev of null; return front. On the right, a deque of two nodes, A and B, each with prev, data and next parts: A's next arrow arcs over to B and B's prev arrow arcs under, back to A; firstNode points at A and lastNode at B. Step one: front equals A, in a dark pill below. Step two: firstNode's arrow swings to B; B's prev still points back at A. Step three: firstNode is not null, so the else runs. Step four: firstNode dot setPrev of null; B's prev part is washed orange, the arrow under the chain goes and B's prev reads null; nothing refers to A's node, and it fades. Step five: return A. Step six: removeFront again, on the deque with only B; front equals B. Step seven: firstNode becomes B's next, which is null; its arrow goes. Step eight: firstNode equals null is true. Step nine: lastNode equals null as well; its arrow goes and B's node fades. Step ten: return B. Step eleven: the front end moved every time; the back end changed only when the deque emptied.
