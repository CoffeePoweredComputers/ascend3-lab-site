What does the last line print?

A recall of a Week 2 peer-instruction question. On the left, a nine-line program that makes a Movie, points a second name at the same one, updates the year through that second name, then points it at a third movie and updates the year again, before printing through the first name. On the right, a summary of what the Movie class offers, and four answers to choose between: in 2021; in 1984; in 2024; in 1984, 2024. On the one step, the four answers return under both columns, A, C and D struck through in turn and B, in 1984, left standing.

your program

  1. Movie film1 = new Movie();
  2. film1.setMovie("Dune", "sci-fi", 2021);
  3. Movie film2 = film1;
  4. film2.updateYear(1984);
  5. Movie film3 = new Movie();
  6. film3.setMovie("Dune", "sci-fi", 2021);
  7. film2 = film3;
  8. film2.updateYear(2024);
  9. film1.print();

class Movie

  • title, genre, year
  • setMovie(title, genre, year)
  • updateYear(year)
  • print() → "title genre movie in year"
  1. … in 2021
  2. … in 1984
  3. … in 2024
  4. … in 1984, 2024
  1. … in 2021
  2. … in 1984
  3. … in 2024
  4. … in 1984, 2024
Answer: B — Dune sci-fi movie in 1984. Show of hands for A–D, then press once for the answer. Line 3 makes film2 refer to the same object as film1, so line 4’s update through film2 is visible through film1. Line 7 makes film2 refer to film3’s object, so line 8 does not affect film1’s object. A assumes film2 got a copy; C assumes the last updateYear reaches everything; D assumes the object keeps its history. Two test questions are this move; a third asks you to write equals for a class like Movie.
