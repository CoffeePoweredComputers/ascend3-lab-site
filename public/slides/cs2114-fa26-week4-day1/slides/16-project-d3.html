Before you go: Project 1 ends next week at your lab with Deliverable 3, and the talk is the point of it. Step 1: ten minutes per group to your lab section, with a live demo; there is no separate writeup, the talk replaces it. What is due with it: working code that does the MVP and survives bad input, JUnit tests that run (one normal and one bad-input case per key method), the source with a line on how to compile and run it, and the slides. Step 2, about two minutes: what you built — the MVP and your system diagram, what changed from your Deliverable 2 spec and why, any stretch goals. Step 3, about two minutes: the demo — one MVP feature working and one bad input handled. Step 4, about two minutes: what went well and what did not, from scope to spec and from spec to code; what scoping means to you now compared with Week 3. Step 5, three to four minutes, the most important part: what you learned about using GenAI. This is why the talks happen in front of each other: every group used LLMs differently and hit different problems, and the section learns more from ten short reports of that than from anything I could say. The ask is one specific thing the other groups may not know yet — a way of prompting that worked well or badly, a wrong answer and how you noticed, a task it helped with that you did not expect (scoping, testing, debugging), or how your use of it changed between Week 3 and now — with the actual prompt or output on the screen. Step 6: due at the start of your lab session; graded EMRN, aim for at least Meets; bring the work ready to defend, the grading session is an oral exam. The full brief, the learning objectives and the EMRN table are on the Project 1: Learning to Scope overview page on Canvas.
