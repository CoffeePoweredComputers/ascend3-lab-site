This is the textbook’s revised design from the 11.2 inheritance material. Step 1: LinkedChainBase is an abstract class that holds the chain, firstNode and numberOfEntries, and the methods every chain-based list needs, such as getEntry and isEmpty. Its two insert methods, addFirstNode and addAfterNode, are protected. A subclass can call them; client code cannot. The slide shows only these members; the textbook’s base has more. Step 2: LListRevised extends the base and adds the public positional methods, add(newPosition, newEntry) and replace(givenPosition, newEntry). It is the ordinary list. Step 3: LinkedSortedList extends the same base. It adds add(newEntry), remove(anEntry) and getPosition(anEntry), and it has no positional add or replace. Step 4: so the call that broke the order on the last slide does not compile against a LinkedSortedList. LListRevised has that method, but the sorted list does not inherit from it; they are siblings under the base. Step 5: inside LinkedSortedList, add(newEntry) finds nodeBefore with getNodeBefore, as last week, and then calls the protected addAfterNode to link the new node in.
