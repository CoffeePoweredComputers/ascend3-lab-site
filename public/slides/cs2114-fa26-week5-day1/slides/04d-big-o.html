COUNTING MOVES · THE NAMES

Three shapes, three names

A graph of moves against n, the number of entries, filling the slide, with dashed guide lines at n equals 4 and n equals 8. Step one: a flat blue line at three moves draws on, labelled add, O of 1, 3 moves, with dots reading 3 at both guides; the caption says constant: double n and the moves stay the same. Step two: a rising maroon line draws on, labelled contains, O of n, n compares, with dots reading 4 and 8; the caption says linear: double n and the moves double. Step three: a maroon curve bends upward and leaves the top of the graph, labelled hasDuplicate, O of n squared, about n squared over 2, with dots reading 6 and 28; the caption says quadratic: double n and the moves go up about four times. Step four: the caption says Big-O keeps the shape and drops the constants, and names the worst case for that n.

The three counts on one picture. Across: n, how many entries. Up: moves. The two dashed lines are the test we ran three times: double n and read the dots. Step 1: add, flat at three; three at four entries, three at eight. Constant, O(1). Step 2: contains, a straight rise; four at four, eight at eight. Linear, O(n). Step 3: hasDuplicate bends up and leaves the graph; six at four, twenty-eight at eight, more than four times. Quadratic, O(n squared). Step 4: two rules. Big-O keeps the shape and drops the constants: three moves and four moves are both O(1); n compares and n over two compares are both O(n). And it names the worst case, the most moves that n can cost, because that is the count that grows. Module 5.1 has the formal definitions; this is what they mean.
