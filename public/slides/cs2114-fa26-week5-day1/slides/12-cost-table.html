The costs in one table

Two tables arriving in turn, then a footnote. Left, Stack: push, pop and peek are each O of 1 for the array with the top at the last occupied index and for the linked chain with the top at the first node. Right, Bag, for a fixed-size array and for a linked chain: add O of 1 and O of 1; contains O of n and O of n; getFrequencyOf O of n and O of n; remove with no argument O of 1 and O of 1; remove of an entry O of n and O of n; toArray O of n and O of n. The footnote names two exceptions the votes found: an array that is not a fixed length makes add O of n on the call that grows it, and an array with the top at index 0 makes pop and push O of n.

The reference card; skip the walk-through if the clock is short, it is in the notes. Step 1: the stack — both of the module’s designs give push, pop and peek in constant time, because both keep the top where nothing has to move. Step 2: the bag from Weeks 3 and 4 — add and the no-argument remove are one move, because a bag has no order and the end is always free; anything that has to find an entry, or visit every entry, is linear, and it is linear on the chain too. Step 3: the two exceptions today found — an array that grows makes add O(n) on the call that grows it, and a top at index 0 makes the stack shift. Next: the homework.
