hasDuplicate: every entry against every other

A walkthrough of a hasDuplicate method under a program-counter bar: an outer loop over i, an inner loop over j from i plus 1, and one compare of contents at i with contents at j. On the right, a four-by-four grid of the pairs for ant, bee, cat and dog, with only the cells above the diagonal drawn, and a large counter labelled compares. Step one: the three cells in ant’s row light in maroon one after another and the counter rolls to 3. Step two: bee’s two cells; the counter reads 5. Step three: cat’s one cell; the counter reads 6. Step four: return false; four entries, six compares. Step five: an eight-by-eight grid appears beside the first with all twenty-eight cells above the diagonal lit and the count 28, and the caption says the moves grow like n squared: quadratic.

Skip this slide if the clock is against you; the next one names the shape anyway. A loop inside a loop: for every entry, compare it with every entry after it. The grid is the honest picture — a cell per pair, a row per outer iteration, only the upper triangle because j starts after i. Step 1: ant against the three after it. Step 2: bee against two. Step 3: cat against one; dog has nobody left. Step 4: no duplicate, return false — six compares for four entries. Step 5: eight entries, twenty-eight compares. Ask: doubling the entries did what to the count? More than quadrupled it. n times n minus one over two — for large n that behaves like n squared, and the shape is called quadratic.
