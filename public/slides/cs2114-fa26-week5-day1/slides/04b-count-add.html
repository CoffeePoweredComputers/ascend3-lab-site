add: the same three moves at any size

A walkthrough of the fixed-size add under a program-counter bar, with short names: the array is a, its count is n, the new entry is x. On the right, the call bag dot add of gnu; a six-cell array holding ant, bee, cat and dog with two free cells; the count and the length; a large counter labelled moves. Step one: ok is set to true. Step two: the count and length take a blue edge and the counter reads 1. Step three: gnu is written into cell 4. Step four: n is struck from 4 to 5; the counter reads 3. Step five: return. Step six: the array grows in place to ten cells, hen, jay, kit and owl fill cells 5 to 8, the count reads 9 and the counter resets to 0. Steps seven to ten: the same four lines run again, yak is written into cell 9 and n goes from 9 to 10, with the counter rolling to 3 again. Step eleven: the caption says the moves do not grow with n: constant.

Same grammar, opposite result. This is the Week 3 add with the names shortened: a is contents, n is numberOfEntries, x is the new entry. A fixed-size array, so a full bag refuses the entry. Step 1: ok true. Step 2: the one compare: is the count equal to the length? Four against six, no. Step 3: the write goes straight to slot four; nobody looks at ant, bee, cat or dog. Step 4: the count goes up. Step 5: return; three moves. Step 6: the bag grows to nine entries in an array of ten, and the same call runs again. Steps 7 to 10: the same three moves. Step 11: nine entries or nine thousand, three moves. Ask: where did n go? Nowhere; it is not in the code. That is what constant means. Next: what happens when a loop sits inside a loop.
