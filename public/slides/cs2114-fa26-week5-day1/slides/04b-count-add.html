add: the same three moves at any size

A walkthrough of the fixed-size ArrayBag add method under a program-counter bar. On the right, the call bag dot add of gnu; a six-cell array holding ant, bee, cat and dog with two free cells; the count numberOfEntries equals 4 and the length 6; and a large counter labelled moves. Step one: result is set to true. Step two: the count and the length take a blue edge and the counter rolls to 1: is the array full? Step three: gnu is written into cell 4, which takes a blue edge; the counter reads 2. Step four: the count is struck through and 5 appears beside it; the counter reads 3. Step five: return true. Step six: a ten-cell array appears underneath with nine entries and kit just written into the last cell, its count 9 struck to 10, and a count of 3 moves beside it; the caption says the moves do not grow with n: constant.

A bag of nine: the same three moves. n never enters it — constant.

Same grammar, opposite result. This is the Week 3 add: a fixed-size array, so a full bag refuses the entry. Step 1: result true. Step 2: the one compare — is the count equal to the length? Four against six, no. Step 3: the write goes straight to slot four; nobody looks at ant, bee, cat or dog. Step 4: the count goes up. Step 5: return — three moves. Step 6: a bag of nine, the same three moves; a bag of nine thousand, the same three. Ask: where did n go? Nowhere — it is not in the code. That is what constant means.
