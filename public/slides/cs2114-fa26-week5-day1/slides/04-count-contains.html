contains: one compare per entry

A walkthrough of contains under a program-counter bar, with short names: the array is a, its count is n, the entry sought is x. On the right, the call bag dot contains of gnu, where gnu is not in the bag; a four-cell array holding ant, bee, cat and dog; the locals; and a large counter labelled compares. Step one: found is false and i is 0. Steps two to five: the bar sits on the loop while each cell in turn takes a maroon edge and the counter rolls from 0 to 4. Step six: return false; four entries, four compares. Step seven: the array grows in place to eight cells, the cells shrinking to make room and eel, fox, hen and jay growing in; the counter resets to 0 and the bar returns to the first two lines. Step eight: the loop runs again and all eight cells take the maroon edge one after another while the counter rolls to 8. Step nine: return false; the caption says twice the entries, twice the compares: linear.

This is the contains the room chose in Week 3, option A, with the names shortened: a is contents, n is numberOfEntries, x is the entry we are looking for. The only new thing is the number on the right. Say what we count: a move is one thing the computer does that we care about, here one call to equals. Step 1: found false, i zero. Steps 2 to 5: press once per entry, ant, bee, cat, dog, and watch the counter; gnu is not in the bag, so no compare succeeds and the loop stops only when i reaches n. Step 6: return false; four entries, four compares. Ask: and if gnu were at index 0? One compare, the best case. We count the worst case, because that is the one that grows. Step 7: the bag doubles in place, same call from the top. Step 8: the loop runs eight times. Step 9: twice the entries, twice the compares. The word for that is linear.
