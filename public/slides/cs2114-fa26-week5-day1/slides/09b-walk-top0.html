Popping index 0 moves every other entry

A walkthrough of pop for an array stack with the top at index 0, under a program-counter bar: read stack at 0, then a loop that copies stack at i plus 1 into stack at i for every i, then the count comes down and the old last slot is cleared. On the right, an orange marker top over cell 0 of a six-cell array holding E, D, C, B, A and a free cell; the count and a local top; a large counter labelled moves. Step one: cell 0 takes a blue edge and top becomes E. Steps two to five: the bar sits on the loop while D, C, B and A each slide one cell to the left and the counter rolls to 5. Step six: the count is struck from 5 to 4. Step seven: cell 4 shows null and goes dashed. Step eight: the four options return under the code, A, C and D struck through and B, O of n, left standing, and the caption says the shift is n minus 1 moves.

Read the top: stack[0] is E. That is what pop will return.

i = 0: stack[0] = stack[1]. D moves up one.

i = 1: C moves up one.

i = 2: B moves up one.

i = 3: A moves up one. Four moves for five entries.

The count comes down: 5 → 4.

The old last slot is cleared.

Seven moves, four of them the shift — and the shift is n − 1. O(n). push shifts too.

Answer: B — and the loop is the argument. Step 1: read the top, E; one move, and if pop stopped here it would be O(1) — that is the A vote. But the top has to be at index 0 afterwards. Steps 2 to 5: the loop, one entry per press — D, C, B, A each move up one slot. Four moves for five entries; n minus one in general, and that is the part that grows. Step 6: the count. Step 7: the old last slot is cleared. Step 8: seven moves, four of them the shift. O(n). Say the mirror out loud: push with the top at index 0 has to shift everything the other way first, so it is O(n) too. Vote 3 moves the top to the other end.
