THE DEAL

No right answer! — guided practice. TAs give feedback, you iterate; you get better at scoping, specifying and building all semester.

We are not grading the sword. We are grading the smith you are becoming.

This slide exists to stop week-3 scope sheets that promise the next Instagram or TikTok — say that out loud, and say the other half too: nobody wants you to build one, and nobody is grading you on it. The scene is last week’s deal drawn as a workshop: on Tuesday the board said “your tutor” and “the apprentices”, and here they are — a master at the anvil and an apprentice watching. The left column is a deck of scope cards, dealt one per step — read each card out loud, do first, don’t second; the don’ts are affectionate, and the dream is never wrong, it is just not three weeks. Step 1 — the hammer comes down as the first pair lands: a text game with character classes, rooms and words loaded from text files you had GenAI generate — a real three-week size, object-oriented by nature, and a malformed file is your bad-input story. Not the next Skyrim. Step 2 — the ornate sword grows in the thought bubble as the second pair lands: a flashcard trainer that reads decks from a file and survives a malformed line. Not the next Duolingo — no accounts, no leaderboards, no phone app. Step 3 — the dream dims and the small orange piece grows in the apprentice’s hand as the third pair lands: a budget tracker, a month of transactions in, overspending flagged. Not the next Venmo — no real money, no database. Point at the shape all three do’s share: a handful of classes with one job each, data coming in from a file, bad input survived — that is the whole technical ask. Say these are calibration, not a menu: your own idea at this size beats any of these. The sheet’s own question — “what do you not know how to do yet?” — is graded on honesty, not ambition; a team that writes “we don’t know how file I/O works yet” is doing the assignment, not confessing. Step 4 — the question marks travel both ways and the last card lands on the pile, THE DEAL — the same words Tuesday’s board used: these projects are apprenticeship-shaped. There is no right answer to hit; the TAs give feedback and you iterate — the grading sessions are conversations, EMRN and oral — and across P1, P2 and P3 you get better at scoping, specifying and implementing, because every project runs the same loop. Treat them as guided practice with a master at the bench, not like the projects you may have met before — a spec handed down and a right answer at the end. The caption is the thesis: we are not grading the sword, we are grading the smith you are becoming. Hand-off: whatever size you scope, Deliverable 3 will ask you to prove it works — the divider next door picks that up: what does a passing test tell you?
