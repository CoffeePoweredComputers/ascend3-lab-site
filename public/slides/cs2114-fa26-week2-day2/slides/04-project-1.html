Step 1 — the figure takes the first waypoint: finish the Bag content pages, sections 1 and 2 at the very least; nothing in the project reads until they are done. Step 2 — this is the one they will skip. The figure stands at the Eclipse window and hands the untouched shell up to Web-CAT; the tick comes back. Submitting an empty shell tells you the whole pipeline works while the stakes are zero; discovering it at midnight in Week 5 is the alternative. Live demo here, on the projector — setup and the first output: the import, the submit and the run, together. Do the demo live rather than describing it, and show the output of the Window example so they know what “working” looks like. Step 3 — read the Window example before writing your own. Step 4 — then write, not before. Start as soon as you can: the part that eats a week is never the algorithm, it is the tooling, and you can have that out of the way today. Step 5 — the report card at the top of the trail: correctness and coverage, both empty, a question mark beside each. Leave the question hanging; Part 2 answers it.
