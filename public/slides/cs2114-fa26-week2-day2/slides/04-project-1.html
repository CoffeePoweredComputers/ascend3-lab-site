Project 1 runs Weeks 3–5, Sep 7–25, and every project this term runs the same loop: scope, then spec, then build. P1 is the lightest of the three; its job is to teach you to define what you are building before you build it, and the scope and the spec count as much as the code, maybe more. Step 1 — three orange busts stand up at the foot of the trail: teams of 3, formed in this week’s lab — orientation, GitHub, and your initial group — and posted on Canvas; check Canvas for your group. The app is your own choosing: a task tracker, a vending-machine simulator, a flashcard quiz, a budget tool, a small text adventure. Keep it buildable in about two weeks of coding; a focused MVP that works beats an ambitious one that does not. Three requirements whatever the idea: object-oriented Java (multiple classes with separate responsibilities, arrays where they fit); GitHub, to collaborate with version control and to turn in; and robust exception handling, which means surviving bad input without crashing (validate, fail gracefully, re-prompt), not an empty try/catch around everything. The grey tick before the first waypoint is Labor Day, Monday Sep 7: Week 3 meets Tue–Fri and Monday lab sections do not meet that day, so confirm the Deliverable 1 hand-in time for your section. Step 2 — the team walks to Week 3 and a one-page sheet grows: the scope document, due at the start of your lab session, answering five questions: what are you building, in a paragraph a non-CS person could understand; what is your MVP; what are the stretch goals; what bad input must it survive; what do you not know how to do yet. Step 3 — Week 4, the specification, 3–5 pages, due at your lab: class design with one job per class; a system diagram of boxes and arrows, required; data and state (the fields, and which collection or structure stores the core data and why); method signatures, the contract a teammate or GenAI builds against; where validation lives; a test plan with one normal case and one bad-input case per public method; division of work; and a revised scope. Step 4 — Week 5, the build, due at your lab: working code that handles bad input, a handful of JUnit tests (at least one normal and one bad-input case per key method), an individual half-to-one-page writeup, and a 3-minute lightning talk in discussion section. Grading is EMRN per deliverable, no points, and the grading sessions with the TAs are oral exams: learn all parts of your system, and do not offload to GenAI without following what it is doing. Everything is due the day of your lab. On GenAI: bring your own idea first, then use it as a brainstorming partner; do not one-shot prompt the assignment; collaborate and discuss first, then critique the GenAI output as a group. Seating: never sit with your project group in lecture; always sit with them in discussion. Step 5 — the test list grows beside the laptop, one normal case and one bad input, and the caption asks what a test actually proves. Leave it hanging. Part 2 answers that.
