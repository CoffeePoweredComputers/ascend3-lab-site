Key takeaway

LLMs can already write items in many formats but few check whether those items measure anything.

Tan, Armoush, Mazzullo, Bulut & Gierl 2025, IJATE 12(2). Scoping review: 831 records screened across seven databases, search closed Aug 1, 2023. Timeline: 2 studies in 2019 → 5 → 9 → 33 (2022 peak) → 11 by Aug 2023. Model use by instance: item generation in 53 of 60 studies — T5 33, GPT 15, BERT 9; pre-generation prep (embedding, key phrases, summarization, prompt engineering) — BERT 20, T5 13, GPT 9; 44 studies used LLMs in one stage only, 3 across all three. Items: 39 constructed-response, 29 selected-response, 8 both. Domains: language learning 24, general knowledge 17, science 9, CS 5, math 4; English in 51 instances of 12 languages. Remaining keyword counts: fairness 3, cognitive level 3, subject-matter experts 3, CTT 1, content specialists 1. Caveat: many keyword hits aren't even about the items (e.g. “inter-annotator reliability”), so the gap is likely wider than the chart shows. Their two fixes: state the assessment purpose and measurement goal up front, and evaluate measurement properties plus pedagogical soundness as a required post-generation stage with SMEs in the loop.

Chart terms: validity — does the item measure what it claims to measure. Reliability — are scores consistent/reproducible (e.g. Cronbach's α). Pedagogical — do the items have teaching value, aligned with learning objectives and feedback. Psychometrics — the field as a whole: validity, reliability, item analysis, scaling. IRT — item response theory, one model family inside psychometrics, modeling P(correct) from student ability and item parameters (what Isley et al. do with the 2PL); classical test theory (CTT, 1 hit here) is the other framework, where difficulty is just proportion correct and discrimination a point-biserial correlation. Item discrimination — how sharply an item separates strong from weak students. Item difficulty — how hard the item is. Bloom's taxonomy — cognitive levels remember → create; nobody classified whether generated items go beyond recall. Read the bars as keyword hits, not audits performed, and note the categories are nested rather than parallel — validity, reliability, difficulty and discrimination all sit inside psychometrics, and difficulty and discrimination are parameters both IRT and CTT estimate — so 2 vs 2 vs 1 vs 0 is not a ranking. The difficulty zero is a phrasing artifact in particular: Tan et al. themselves describe post-generation studies filtering items on “quality and difficulty” (p. 323), those studies just never wrote the two-word phrase. The defensible claim is that almost nobody reports measurement properties, not that exactly one study computed a discrimination index.
