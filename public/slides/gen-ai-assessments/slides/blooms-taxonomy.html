1 · Remember

Q1. len(scores) returns…

  • the largest score
  • the number of items in the list
  • the last score
  • the sum of the scores

B — one stated fact, straight from memory.

2 · Understand

Q2. In one sentence, what does this loop do?

total = 0
for s in scores:
    total += s

It folds every score into a running total — the idea, restated in your own words.

3 · Apply

Q3. Rewrite this loop to sum only the even-indexed scores.

for s in scores:
    total += s

for s in scores[::2]: total += s — known procedure, new input.

4 · Analyze

Q4. This works, but it is O(n²). Why?

for s in scores:
    if scores.count(s) > 1:
        flagged.append(s)

Taken apart: count() rescans the list on every pass — a loop hidden inside the loop.

5 · Evaluate

Q5. Which version is preferable?

A: total = sum(scores)
B: total = sum(check(s) for s in scores)

B — equal totals, but it fails loudly on a bad score. Judged against criteria.
