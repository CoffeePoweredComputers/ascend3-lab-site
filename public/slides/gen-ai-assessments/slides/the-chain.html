  • Kane, M. T. (2013). Validating the interpretations and uses of test scores. Journal of Educational Measurement, 50(1), 1–73.
* Validity is not a property of a test — it is a property of a proposed interpretation or use of the scores, so the thing being validated is a claim so the question is "how do we make a valid inference from a test score to a claim"? * Historically validity is divided into three substitutable types (content, criterion, construct) and treated reliability as a separate concern; Messick (1995) called that fragmented and folded them into one construct validity with six aspects, adding the consequences of score use; Kane then recast validation as building and evaluating an argument rather than collecting a type of evidence. [ walk through Kane's argument based validity ] * I am showing Kane’s chain because it turns an abstract word into four inferences you can actually check, and because every one of them has to hold for the conclusion to hold — which is why good item statistics on their own do not make a test valid (Kane, 2013, p. 3: evidence of reliability “rarely is sufficient for validity”).
