A BinaryNode holds its data and two references, leftChild and rightChild.

A BinaryTree has one field, root, a reference to the top node.

root refers to the node that holds 10.

The leftChild of 10 refers to the node that holds 3.

The rightChild of 10 refers to the node that holds 12.

7 is the rightChild of 3, and 20 is the rightChild of 12. Every other reference is null.

The tree itself keeps only one reference, root, and every other node is reached through leftChild and rightChild.

These are the fields from the ProgrammingTrees slides in the 13.2 module; the methods come next. Step 1: a BinaryNode holds its data and two references to other BinaryNodes, leftChild and rightChild. A list node had only one, next. Step 2: a BinaryTree has one field, root. Step 3: root refers to the top node, here the node holding 10. Step 4: 10’s leftChild refers to the node holding 3. Step 5: its rightChild refers to 12. Step 6: 7 is 3’s right child and 20 is 12’s right child; every other reference is null, so 3 has no left child, 12 has no left child, and 7 and 20 are leaves. Step 7: the tree object keeps only root. To reach 7 the code must go root, then leftChild, then rightChild; there is no array and no index.
