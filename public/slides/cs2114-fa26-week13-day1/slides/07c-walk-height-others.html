The other options of Vote 1, each tried on the two trees from the last slide. A card on the right says what they need: n equals 7 has height 3, and n equals 15 has height 4. Step one, a card for option A, log base 2 of n: for n equals 7 it gives about 2.8, and for 15 about 3.9; neither is a whole number, because n is one short of a power of 2. Step two, a card for option B, log base 2 of n minus 1: about 2.6 and about 3.8; inside the log, n minus 1 is two short of a power of 2. Answer: D. Each other option tried on the two trees from the last slide, 7 nodes with height 3 and 15 nodes with height 4. Step 1: A, log2(n). log2(7) is about 2.8 and log2(15) about 3.9. A height is a whole number of levels, and these come out just short, because n is always one less than a power of 2 in this shape. Step 2: B, log2(n − 1), is further off, about 2.6 and 3.8. Step 3: C, E and F are linear in n. For 7 nodes they give 15, 13 and 14, for 15 nodes 31, 29 and 30. They grow as fast as the number of nodes, but each new level doubles the nodes and adds only 1 to the height, so the height must grow far more slowly than n. Students who chose these may have mixed up the node count with the height; note that the PL version of this question writes them as powers of 2, which is the formula for the node count turned the wrong way round. Step 4: D is the only option that gives 3 and 4. Next is Vote 2.
