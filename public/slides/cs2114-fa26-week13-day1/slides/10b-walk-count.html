getNumberOfNodes adds 1 to the counts of the two subtrees

A walkthrough of getNumberOfNodes on the five-value tree, under a program-counter bar. The method sets leftNumber and rightNumber to 0; if the node’s left child is not null, leftNumber becomes getNumberOfNodes of the left child; if the right child is not null, rightNumber becomes getNumberOfNodes of the right child; it returns 1 plus leftNumber plus rightNumber. Each call starts both counts at 0, makes a recursive call only for a child that exists, and returns itself, the 1, plus what its two calls returned. Step 1: the call on 10. Step 2: 10 has a left child, 3, so the call on 3 begins, and the call on 10 waits on line 5 for the answer. Step 3: 3 has no left child, so its leftNumber stays 0; it has a right child, 7, so the call on 7 begins. Step 4: 7 has no children; neither if runs and it returns 1 + 0 + 0 = 1. That is the first node counted. Step 5: the call on 7 is finished and removed. Its 1 goes to the call on 3 as rightNumber, and 3 returns 1 + 0 + 1 = 2. Step 6: the call on 3 is removed and its 2 becomes the leftNumber of 10. 10 has a right child, so the call on 12 begins. Step 7: 12 has no left child; the call on 20 returns 1. Step 8: 12 returns 1 + 0 + 1 = 2. Step 9: 10 returns 1 + 2 + 2 = 5. Step 10: every node was visited by exactly one call, and each call counts only itself; the children’s calls count the rest.
