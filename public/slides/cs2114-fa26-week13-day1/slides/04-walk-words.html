A tree is built from a root, edges, parents and children

The course’s example tree built in the centre of the slide, with each term written on the part of the tree it names and one sentence under the tree per press. Step one: a circle labelled A appears with the word root above it; A is the root and has no parent. Step two: edges draw down from A to B on the left and C on the right; the word edge points to the line from A to B, the word parent is beside A and the word child is beside B and beside C. Step three: a dashed blue line joins B and C under the word siblings. Step four: D and E appear under B; B is a child of A and also the parent of D and E. Step five: H appears as C’s right child, and C is outlined; C has one child, and a binary tree node has 0, 1 or 2 children. Step six: F and G appear under E. Step seven: D, F, G and H take a pale blue fill and the word leaf appears under each of them. Step eight: A, B, C and E take an orange outline, and the words inner node appear beside C. Step nine: a dashed blue box is drawn round B, D, E, F and G, labelled A’s left subtree; it is itself a binary tree. Step ten, in maroon: a binary tree is a root with a left subtree and a right subtree, either of which may be empty.

A is the root, the one node that has no parent.

An edge joins a parent to a child. B and C are A’s children.

B and C are siblings, because they have the same parent.

D and E are B’s children, so B is both a child of A and the parent of D and E.

C has only one child; a node in a binary tree may have 0, 1 or 2 children.

F and G are E’s children.

A leaf is a node with no children, so D, F, G and H are leaves.

The other nodes, A, B, C and E, each have at least one child and are called inner nodes.

B with everything under it is A’s left subtree, and it is itself a binary tree.

A binary tree is a root with a left subtree and a right subtree, either of which may be empty.

The terms come from the 13.2 module, and each one appears on the part of the tree it names. The tree is the course’s example tree from the traversal video, which Day 2 walks again. Step 1: A is the root, the one node with no parent. Step 2: an edge joins a parent to a child. A is the parent of B and C, and B and C are A’s children. Step 3: B and C are siblings, because they have the same parent. Step 4: D and E are B’s children, so B is both a child, of A, and a parent, of D and E. Step 5: H hangs on C’s right, and C has no left child. In a binary tree every node has 0, 1 or 2 children, and each child is either the left child or the right child. Step 6: F and G are E’s children. Step 7: a leaf is a node with no children: D, F, G and H. Step 8: every other node is an inner node, A, B, C and E. Step 9: B with everything under it, B, D, E, F and G, is A’s left subtree. It is a binary tree in its own right with B as its root. C and H are A’s right subtree. Step 10: that is the recursive description the code in Part 3 relies on. A binary tree is a root with a left subtree and a right subtree, and either subtree may be empty.
