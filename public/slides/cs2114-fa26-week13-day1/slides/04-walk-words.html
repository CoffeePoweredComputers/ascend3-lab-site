A tree is built from a root, edges, parents and children

The course’s example tree built one word at a time, with a growing list of the words on the right and one sentence under them per press. Step one: a circle labelled A appears; it is the root and has no parent. Step two: edges draw down from A to B on the left and C on the right; an edge joins a parent to a child, and B and C are A’s children. Step three: B and C are outlined in blue; they are siblings, with the same parent. Step four: D and E appear under B; B is a child of A and also the parent of D and E. Step five: H appears as C’s right child, and C is outlined; C has one child, and a binary tree node has 0, 1 or 2 children. Step six: F and G appear under E. Step seven: D, F, G and H take a pale blue fill; a leaf has no children. Step eight: A, B, C and E take an orange outline; they are the inner nodes. Step nine: a dashed blue box is drawn round B, D, E, F and G; that is A’s left subtree, and it is itself a binary tree. Step ten, in maroon: a binary tree is a root with a left subtree and a right subtree, either of which may be empty.

A is the root, the one node that has no parent.

An edge joins a parent to a child. B and C are A’s children.

B and C are siblings, because they have the same parent.

D and E are B’s children, so B is both a child of A and the parent of D and E.

C has only one child; a node in a binary tree may have 0, 1 or 2 children.

F and G are E’s children.

A leaf is a node with no children, so D, F, G and H are leaves.

The other nodes, A, B, C and E, each have at least one child and are called inner nodes.

B with everything under it is A’s left subtree, and it is itself a binary tree.

A binary tree is a root with a left subtree and a right subtree, either of which may be empty.
