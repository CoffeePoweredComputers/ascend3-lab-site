These are the level counts from slide 06: 1, 2 and 4.

Added up, the height-3 tree has 1 + 2 + 4 = 7 nodes.

One more level doubles the last one, so level 4 holds 8 leaves.

The height-4 tree has 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 = 15 nodes.

Each total is one less than the next power of 2, so 7 = 23 − 1 and 15 = 24 − 1.

A tree of height h in this shape has n = 2h − 1 nodes.

Adding 1 to both sides gives n + 1 = 2h, so h = log2(n + 1).

The height is log2(n + 1). For 7 nodes that is 3, and for 15 nodes it is 4.

Answer: D — log2(n+1) [key from the PrairieLearn twin tree_formula; confirm in Runestone’s results view]. Step 1: the per-level counts from slide 06, 1, 2 and 4. Step 2: add them up; the height-3 tree has 7 nodes. Step 3: grow the tree one level. Every leaf takes two children, so the new level holds 8, twice the level above. Step 4: the height-4 tree has 15 nodes. Step 5: 7 is one less than 8, which is 2 to the 3, and 15 is one less than 16, which is 2 to the 4. The pattern holds at every height because level k holds 2 to the k minus 1 nodes, and 1 + 2 + 4 + ... + 2 to the h minus 1 is 2 to the h minus 1. Step 6: so a tree of height h in this shape has n = 2^h − 1 nodes. Step 7: the vote gives n and asks for h. Add 1 to both sides, n + 1 = 2^h, and take the base-2 logarithm: h = log2(n + 1). Step 8: check it on the two trees. log2(8) = 3 and log2(16) = 4. Next we see why the other five options do not fit.
