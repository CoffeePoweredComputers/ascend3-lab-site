The TERM comes last: walkthrough first, name at the bottom on the final click.] SETUP (before click 1, over the title): Programming has been an English-language activity. The keywords are English. Stack Overflow, MDN, the docs — English first, translations lag by years. It would be easy to assume natural-language programming just inherits that. It doesn't: these models are trained on text in hundreds of languages. English is a default now, not a requirement. [Guo, CHI 2018, if asked.] THE FOIL (still before click 1 — this is what makes "one repertoire" mean anything): Applied linguistics has two accounts of how multilinguals work. The older one is code-switching: separate language systems, and you toggle between them like changing radio stations. Translanguaging says that's wrong — there are no separate systems. There's one repertoire, and you select features from it without regard for where the named-language boundaries fall. That's the framing I find more powerful, and honestly more interesting.
