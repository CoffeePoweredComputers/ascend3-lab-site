[0:45 · 2 clicks · closes Branch I] Lead with the node on the right — this is Fariha's program, and it starts from the general question: how do we design tools and pedagogy for linguistically diverse classrooms? Answering it takes understanding two things. (1) The theory. What theories of language and its role in learning already exist? Applied linguistics, second-language acquisition and multilingual education have been at this for fifty years, and computing education has borrowed almost none of it — the question is what actually transfers when the thing being learned is code rather than an essay. Those borrowed concepts are what's flowing down the indigo stream. The review is a preprint — QR's on the slide. (2) The practice. How are instructors already navigating this space — in computing classrooms in countries like India and Pakistan, where many different languages are spoken? The map is the argument: every state and province wears its own dominant language — 121 languages in India by the census, around seventy in Pakistan — and the quilt doesn't stop at the border (both Punjabs are one color). Interviews with CS faculty; phenomenography, so what we're after is the qualitatively different ways instructors CONCEPTUALIZE linguistic diversity, not a tally of which languages get spoken. And this is not bilingual teaching: one classroom can hold six first languages, an English-medium mandate, and an instructor whose own strongest language is neither — those are the languages flowing down the pink stream. Close on the node again: both studies feed the same design question — which natural language, and whose, is a pedagogy question and a tool-design question, not a footnote.
