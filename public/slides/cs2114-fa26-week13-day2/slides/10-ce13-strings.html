CE 13 writes each traversal as a string with parentheses

How Challenge Exercise 13 writes a traversal, built in five presses on a three-node tree: A with left child B and right child C. Press one: the string (B) appears under B. Press two: (C) appears under C. Press three: copies of (B) and (C) travel up into a row labelled inorder, and parentheses and A fill in around them, so the row reads ((B)A(C)). Press four: two more rows appear, preorder (A(B)(C)) and postorder ((B)(C)A). Press five: a card says that size() is Day 1’s count of nodes, 3 for this tree, and that height() counts levels, so a leaf is 1 and this tree is 2; the caption says that CE 13 asks for the three traversal strings, the size and the height.

Challenge Exercise 13, Binary Tree, is due Friday Nov 20. In CE 13 the BinaryTree class is its own node: getElement, getLeft and getRight. It asks for size(), height(), and three string methods, toPreOrderString, toInOrderString and toPostOrderString, which write the traversal with parentheses. Step 1: a leaf is its letter in parentheses, (B). Step 2: (C). Step 3: the inorder string of A puts B’s string, then A, then C’s string, and wraps the lot in one more pair: ((B)A(C)). Every call does the same with its children’s strings, so the method has the same shape as the inorder walk on slide 04b, with the visit replaced by adding the letter. Step 4: preorder, (A(B)(C)), and postorder, ((B)(C)A): the same pieces, with the letter moved to the front or to the back, as on slides 04 and 04c. Step 5: size() is the count from Day 1, the node plus the sizes of its two subtrees. height() counts levels, as the votes did: a leaf is 1. The module’s ProgrammingTrees slide counts edges instead, where a leaf is 0; CE 13 uses levels.
