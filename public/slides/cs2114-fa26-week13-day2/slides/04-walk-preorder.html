Preorder visits each node before its children

A walkthrough of preorder on the tree from Day 1, under a program-counter bar. The editor holds preorder of node: if node is not null, visit the node’s data, then call preorder on its left child, then on its right child. On the right, the tree: A has children B and C, B has children D and E, E has children F and G, and C has only a right child, H. A blue ring marks the node of the running call, the call stack grows from the bottom up beside the tree, and an output plate sits underneath. Step one: preorder of A prints A. Step two: A’s call waits on line 4 and preorder of B prints B. Step three: D is printed. Step four: D’s two calls on null return and D’s box fades. Step five: B’s call waits on line 5 and E is printed. Step six: F is printed. Step seven: F’s box fades and G is printed. Step eight: G, E and B finish and C is printed. Step nine: H is printed. Step ten: every call has returned, and the plate reads A B D E F G C H: root, then left, then right.

preorder: the standard recursive form

  1. void preorder(BinaryNode<T> node) {
  2. if (node != null) {
  3. visit(node.getData());
  4. preorder(node.getLeftChild());
  5. preorder(node.getRightChild());
  6. }
  7. }

preorder is called on the root, A. A is not null, so A is visited and printed first.

A’s call reaches line 4 and calls preorder on its left child, B. B is printed before anything under it.

B’s call does the same with its left child, D, and D is printed.

Both of D’s children are null. Those two calls return at once, and D’s call is finished.

B’s call reaches line 5 and calls preorder on its right child, E. E is printed.

E’s call goes left to F, and F is printed.

F’s calls on null return, so F’s call is finished. E’s call goes right to G, and G is printed.

G, E and B finish in turn. A’s call reaches line 5 and goes right to C, and C is printed.

C’s left child is null, so that call returns at once. C’s call goes right to H, and H is printed.

The root first, then the left subtree, then the right: A B D E F G C H.

This is the standard recursive preorder, written in the course’s style for this deck; the 13.2 slides name the orders but show no traversal code, so say that the code is ours. visit stands for whatever the traversal does with a node; here it prints the letter. The tree is the one from the module video: A, with B and C under it, D and E under B, F and G under E, and H as C’s right child. Step 1: the call on A prints A before doing anything else. Step 2: A’s call is now waiting on line 4, the call on its left child, and the call on B prints B. Step 3: the call on D prints D. Step 4: D has no children, so both of its calls get null, fail the if, and return. D’s call is done and its box goes away. B’s call is running again. Step 5: B’s call moves on to line 5 and calls on E, which prints E. Step 6: E’s left call prints F. Step 7: F’s null calls return, and E’s right call prints G. Step 8: G finishes, which finishes E, which finishes B. Back in A’s call, line 5 calls on C, which prints C. Step 9: C’s left call gets null and returns; its right call prints H. Step 10: everything returns. A B D E F G C H. Each node is printed when its call starts, before either child. Ask the room: what changes if the visit line moves down one? That is the next slide.
