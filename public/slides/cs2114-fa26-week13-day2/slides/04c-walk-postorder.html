Postorder visits each node after both of its subtrees

A walkthrough of postorder on the same tree, under a program-counter bar. The editor holds postorder of node: if node is not null, the visit line sits after the calls on the left child and the right child. On the right, the tree from the previous slide with a blue ring that moves to each node as it is printed, and an output plate under the tree. There is no call stack on this slide. Step one: the calls go left from A to B to D, and D is printed. Step two: F is printed. Step three: G is printed. Step four: E is printed after both of its children. Step five: B is printed. Step six: the calls go right to C and then to H, and H is printed. Step seven: C is printed. Step eight: A is printed last. Step nine: the plate reads D F G E B H C A: left, then right, then root.

postorder: visit after both calls

  1. void postorder(BinaryNode<T> node) {
  2. if (node != null) {
  3. postorder(node.getLeftChild());
  4. postorder(node.getRightChild());
  5. visit(node.getData());
  6. }
  7. }

A’s call goes left to B, and B’s call goes left to D. Both of D’s calls return at once, so D is printed.

B’s call goes right to E, and E’s call goes left to F. F has no children, so F is printed.

E’s call goes right to G. G has no children, so G is printed.

Both of E’s calls have returned, so E is printed.

Both of B’s calls have returned, so B is printed.

A’s call goes right to C. C’s left call returns at once, and its right call goes to H. H has no children, so H is printed.

Both of C’s calls have returned, so C is printed.

Both of A’s calls have returned, so A, the root, is printed last.

The left subtree, then the right subtree, then the root, which is therefore printed last.

The visit line is now after both recursive calls. Again no stack on the slide. Step 1: the calls go left from A to B to D. D’s two calls on null return, and then D is printed. Step 2: B goes right to E, E goes left to F, and F is printed. Step 3: G. Step 4: E is printed only once both of its children are done. Step 5: B, once D and E are done. Step 6: A goes right to C; C’s left call gets null, its right call reaches H, and H is printed. Step 7: C. Step 8: A is printed last. Step 9: D F G E B H C A. A node is printed after everything under it, so the root always comes last in a postorder listing, and the root always comes first in a preorder listing.
