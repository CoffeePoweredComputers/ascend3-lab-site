Level order visits the tree one level at a time

A walkthrough of level order on the same tree, under a program-counter bar. The editor holds a loop: enqueue the root; while the queue is not empty, dequeue a node, visit it, and enqueue its left child and then its right child when they are not null. On the right, the tree above a queue of five cells with its front on the left, and an output plate. Step one: A is enqueued. Steps two to nine: one node is dequeued per press, printed, and its children travel from the tree into the back of the queue: A, then B and C, then D, E and H, then F and G. Step ten: the queue is empty, and the plate reads A, then B and C, then D, E and H, then F and G: one level at a time, with a queue instead of the call stack.

level order, with a queue

  1. queue.enqueue(root);
  2. while (!queue.isEmpty()) {
  3. node = queue.dequeue();
  4. visit(node.getData());
  5. if (node.getLeftChild() != null)
  6. queue.enqueue(node.getLeftChild());
  7. if (node.getRightChild() != null)
  8. queue.enqueue(node.getRightChild());
  9. }

queue.enqueue(root) puts A in the queue. The front of the queue is on the left.

A is dequeued and printed, and its children B and C are enqueued.

B is dequeued and printed. Its children D and E go in behind C.

C is dequeued and printed. C has only a right child, so H alone is enqueued.

D is dequeued and printed. D has no children, so nothing is enqueued.

E is dequeued and printed. Its children F and G go in behind H.

H is dequeued and printed. H has no children.

F is dequeued and printed. F has no children.

G is dequeued and printed. The queue is now empty, so the loop ends.

Level order visits one level at a time, and it uses a queue instead of the call stack.

Level order is the fourth order in the module video: the root, then everything on level 2 from left to right, then level 3, and so on. It does not fit the recursive pattern, because the next node to visit is often in a different subtree. The code is ours, in the course’s style: node is a local BinaryNode reference and queue is any queue of nodes, such as Week 8’s LinkedQueue. Step 1: the root goes in. Step 2: A comes out of the front and is printed; its children B and C go in at the back, left first. Step 3: B comes out; D and E go in behind C. Step 4: C comes out; it has only a right child, so H alone goes in. Step 5: D comes out with nothing to add. Step 6: E comes out; F and G go in behind H. Steps 7 to 9: H, F and G come out. Step 10: the queue is empty and the loop ends. The plate holds level 1, then level 2, then level 3, then level 4: A; B and C; D, E and H; F and G. The queue is first in, first out, so every node on one level is enqueued before any node on the next level comes out. The call stack is last in, first out, which is why the recursive orders go deep first. Next come the peer-instruction routine and the first vote.
