Late on a team is not a private problem.

Homework and the challenge exercise open Monday — start them that day, not Thursday night. The days are suggestions — the point is that "early" has a date on it. The project row sets up the collaborator slide later: a team's clock starts the day the deliverable is released. Steps 1–4: one row each; read the span aloud as start → due. The challenge exercise is due Friday, same as the homework — the two bars line up. Step 5: that orange bar is three people's pieces — you Mon–Tue, a teammate Wed–Thu, a teammate Fri–Sun — each chained to the one before, finishing exactly at Sunday. Step 6: you start Tuesday instead of Monday; the whole chain shifts and the last piece runs past Sunday. Your teammates cannot build on work that does not exist yet — one late start costs the whole team its week. Say that "you start a day late" and the three-way hand-off are an illustration of the dependency, not a course rule or a real team workflow — real teams parallelise and integrate.
