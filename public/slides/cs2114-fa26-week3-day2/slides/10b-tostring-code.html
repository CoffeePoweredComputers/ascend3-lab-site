A walkthrough of the toString plan. On the left, the stub from the question in an editor: the Override annotation, the method signature, the TODO comment and return null. Between the signature and the TODO, plan lines open one at a time, diff-style with a plus sign in the gutter: the result starts as an opening bracket; for i from 0 up to numberOfEntries minus 1, append contents of i, then a comma and a space unless i is the last; close with a bracket. The plan, not the Java — PrairieLearn grades the Java, and the comment lines are the shape any correct answer has. Step 1 — the result starts as one bracket; say the word “starts”: whatever they build, it opens before the loop. Step 2 — the loop: i from 0 up to numberOfEntries minus 1. Point at the row: five filled cells light up, the three dashed ones and the twenty behind them never come up. This is the bound the whole hour has been about. Step 3 — the fencepost: every item but the last is followed by a comma and a space; the four separators go blue. Either test for the last index, or add the separator before every item after the first — both are fine, and both are one comparison. Step 4 — the close bracket, and the string is exactly the example’s. Step 5 — the empty bag: numberOfEntries is 0, the loop runs zero times, and the two brackets are all that is left — a correct loop gets this case for free, which is the point of asking about it. Step 6 — the two wrong bounds, and neither throws: i <= numberOfEntries prints one extra null, i < contents.length prints twenty. Here the array has room, so the mistake is silent and shows up as output rather than as an exception. Plant: test with a bag that is not full — a full bag hides the bound.
