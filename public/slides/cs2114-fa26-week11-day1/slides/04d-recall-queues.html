RECALL · WEEK 8 · QUEUES AND DEQUES

Which front and rear are legal?

A recall of a Week 8 peer-instruction question. Under the title, the question stem in muted type: suppose we have a Queue implemented with a circular array with one unused slot. The capacity of the queue is 10 and the size is 5. Which are legal values for front and rear? Below it, two panels side by side. Left, a blank array of eleven slots numbered 0 to 10. Right, the five options as rows, each led by a maroon disc with its letter: A, front 0, rear 5; B, front 4, rear 9; C, front 7, rear 2; D, front 9, rear 2; E, all of the above. On the one step, five short verdicts appear under both panels, each giving how many entries the option holds: A 6, B 6, C 7, D 5, E only one option holds 5. A, B, C and E are struck through in turn and D is left standing.

Answer: D — front 9, rear 2 [key from the PrairieLearn twin u10q08_circulararrayqueue_b, which is this question verbatim; confirm in Runestone’s results view]. Show of hands, then the answer. Capacity 10 with one unused slot is an array of 11, indexes 0 to 10. Front and rear are frontIndex and backIndex, and both are the index of an entry, so count the slots from front to rear, both included, wrapping from 10 to 0. A, 0 to 5, is six slots. B, 4 to 9, is six slots. C, 7 to 2, is 7, 8, 9, 10, 0, 1, 2: seven slots. D, 9 to 2, is 9, 10, 0, 1, 2: five slots, the size. E is wrong because only D holds five.
