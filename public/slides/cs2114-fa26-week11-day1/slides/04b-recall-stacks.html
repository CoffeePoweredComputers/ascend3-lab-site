What is on the stack at the end?

A recall of a Week 6 peer-instruction question. Under the title, the stem in muted type: imagine you have an empty stack of strings named stack; what are the contents of the stack after the following operations are executed, assuming the leftmost element in each answer choice represents the top of the stack; assume that the pop method returns the element that was popped. Below it, two panels. Left, an editor with eight lines: push K, push P, push G, push R, String str equals stack dot pop, stack dot pop, push V, push str. Right, the four options as rows, each led by a maroon disc with its letter: A, P V G P; B, K P V R; C, R V P K; D, G V P K. On the one step, four short verdicts appear under the options: A, G was popped; B, bottom first; C, R V P K; D, str is R. A, B and D are struck through in turn and C is left standing.

Imagine you have an empty stack of strings named stack. What are the contents of the stack after the following operations are executed, assuming the leftmost element in each answer choice represents the top of the stack?. (Assume that the pop method returns the element that was popped.)

eight operations on an empty stack

  1. stack.push("K");
  2. stack.push("P");
  3. stack.push("G");
  4. stack.push("R");
  5. String str = stack.pop();
  6. stack.pop();
  7. stack.push("V");
  8. stack.push(str);
  1. A

    P V G P

  2. B

    K P V R

  3. C

    R V P K

  4. D

    G V P K

  1. G popped
  2. bottom up
  3. R V P K
  4. str is R
Answer: C — R V P K, top first [key from the source deck W6D1 05b, by tracing; confirm in Runestone’s results view]. Show of hands, then the answer. After the four pushes the stack is R G P K from the top. The first pop returns R and str keeps it. The second pop removes G, and nothing keeps it. Then V goes on top, and push(str) puts R on top again: R V P K. A has G, which was popped, and P twice. B is the right four letters in the wrong order: it lists the stack from the bottom. D puts G back on the stack, as if str held the second pop; str holds the first pop, R. This topic had three questions on Test 2. Next: recursion.
