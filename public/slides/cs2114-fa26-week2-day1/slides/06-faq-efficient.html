FAQ

Q: Wouldn’t it be more efficient to just tell us the right answer?

A: Have you had a yoga class where you just listen to the person telling what you could do?

A yoga class, drawn. Four students sit cross-legged on their mats, watching attentively, while the instructor stands on a mat at the right in a full warrior pose, in orange, talking. The title asks the question, and the answer under it is another question: have you had a yoga class where you just listen to the person telling what you could do? Then the punchline lands — two words, me neither — and the room inverts: the instructor comes out of the pose and simply stands, going grey, while all four students rise into the pose themselves, in orange, one after another. Last, a line notes that you have to work your own muscle.

Me neither.

To learn it, you have to work your own muscle.

This is the question that arrives in office hours in Week 4, so answer it now. Ask it straight, then wait — let them picture the yoga class where the instructor does every pose at the front while the class watches attentively. Step 1: “Me neither.” Watch the orange move. Watching someone else write correct code feels like learning and is not, the same way watching someone else stretch is not stretching. This is also the answer to “why don’t you just lecture”: the modules on PrairieLearn are the reading, and lecture is the part the reading cannot do for you.
