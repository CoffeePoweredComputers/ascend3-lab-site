What I do, and what you do

One room, two sides of a deal. On the left, a board and a figure standing at it. First a maroon question mark grows on the board and three lines appear beneath the figure: pose the problem, guide you in, answer your questions. Then the right-hand side answers: three students each grow an orange square of their own work, arrows draw between them so each square feeds the next person, and each student is labelled — prepare, engage, ask and offer. Last the whole scene blurs behind a veil and one line is left standing: I expect every member of this class to contribute to a positive environment of mutual aid and cooperation.

I expect every member of this class to contribute to a positive environment of mutual aid and cooperation.

My side, step 1: think of me as your tutor. I pose the problems that set up your exploration, guide you into the concepts rather than hand them over, and answer your questions — and I do not spend the lecture reading the textbook to you in slightly different words. The modules on PrairieLearn are the reading. Your side, step 2: stay ahead of the modules, because lecture assumes you have met the words already; turn the ideas upside down with your neighbours and ask what the common mistake would be; and seek help and offer it, in class, on Piazza, in office hours, in lab — both directions count. The arrows are why: explaining it is how you find out whether you actually knew it, and your understanding is the next person’s input. Step 3: read the card verbatim. It is the one line here that is a rule rather than advice.
