Three ways a group discussion goes well

Three cards, dealt one at a time, each carrying a drawing. First, take turns going first: three people in a ring, one holding a solid speech bubble while the other two hold dashed outlines of the same bubble, and an arrow curving round the ring. Second, don’t stop when you agree: four answer options, one ticked, and the other three struck through — the work that is left after you agree. Third, say it in your own words: one person’s filled speech bubble, and the identical bubble redrawn as an outline over the person beside them.

  • Take turns going first

  • Don’t stop when you agree

  • Say it in your own words

Tip 1 — take turns being the first one to talk. The same person going first every time is how a group stops being three people. Tip 2 is the one that pays: once you all agree on the answer, don’t stop. Go over each wrong answer and say why it is wrong, and why somebody might have been tempted by it — the wrong answers are written to match specific misconceptions, so naming them is most of the learning. That is what the three strikes are: the work that is left after the tick. Tip 3 — say it yourself even if a group-mate said it perfectly. “So what I think you said was…” It feels pointless and it is retrieval practice; your brain remembers it better when the words are yours.
