What does the last line print?

A peer-instruction question. On the left, a nine-line program that makes a Movie, points a second name at the same one, updates the year through that second name, then points it at a third movie and updates the year again, before printing through the first name. On the right, a summary of what the Movie class offers, and four answers to choose between.

your program

  1. Movie film1 = new Movie();
  2. film1.setMovie("Dune", "sci-fi", 2021);
  3. Movie film2 = film1;
  4. film2.updateYear(1984);
  5. Movie film3 = new Movie();
  6. film3.setMovie("Dune", "sci-fi", 2021);
  7. film2 = film3;
  8. film2.updateYear(2024);
  9. film1.print();

class Movie

  • title, genre, year
  • setMovie(title, genre, year)
  • updateYear(year)
  • print() → "title genre movie in year"
  1. … in 2021
  2. … in 1984
  3. … in 2024
  4. … in 1984, 2024
Answer: B — Dune sci-fi movie in 1984. Line 3 gives the same object a second name, so line 4 writes 1984 into the object film1 is looking at. Line 7 re-points film2 at film3’s object; from there on film2 has nothing to do with film1, so line 8 never touches it. Do not walk it through before the second vote. Wrong answers to name afterwards: A assumes film2 got a copy; C assumes the last updateYear reaches everything; D assumes the object remembers its history.
