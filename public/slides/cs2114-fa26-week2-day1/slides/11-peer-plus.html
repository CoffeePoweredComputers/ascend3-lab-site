How a vote runs

Five numbered stations arranged in a ring, arriving one at a time with an arc drawing between each and the next: a hard question, you answer alone, you argue it out, you answer again, we settle it. Last, one more arc draws in orange, from we settle it back round to a hard question — the loop closing.

Wait until laptops are actually open before starting question 1. From the Canvas home page, open the Week 2 assignment “Peer Instruction W02D1” — it opens in Runestone. Station 2 is the one worth policing on the first vote of the semester — work it through silently, on your own, and answer: if they talk before the first answer, the second vote measures nothing. That is why it is the orange one. Station 4: change your answer if the discussion changed your mind; credit is for participating in both rounds, not for being right. Station 5: we go through it together, wrong answers included — the wrong answers are written to match specific misconceptions, so naming why each is wrong is most of the learning. Step 6, the orange arc: this is not a one-off, it is how every lecture runs from here.
