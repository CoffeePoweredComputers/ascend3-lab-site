The order is the content, and it is the same picture as equals() four slides back — say so. Gates 1 and 2 are where the learning is; gate 3 is a tool you reach for once you already know what you are stuck on. Read the gates as a list of capabilities rather than prohibitions: every one is something they will be asked to do in a room with no laptop — trace code, draw the picture, write from scratch. The debugger and Python Tutor answer the same questions an LLM would, faster, and about their actual code. Gate 3’s exit is the only one that is not “unstuck”: it drops you into gate 4. Whatever the LLM hands you, you understand it and then you bring it to us — office hours, lab, Piazza — and verify it before it goes anywhere near your project. Point out that this costs nothing and is faster than they think: a UTA in lab looks at their actual project and the LLM’s actual answer, which no chatbot does. And you document the use, exactly as the syllabus and each assignment describe; that line is on the rule slide, not here. Step 5 is the practical reason for the order: an answer that uses a construct we have not taught leaves you with more to untangle, not less.
