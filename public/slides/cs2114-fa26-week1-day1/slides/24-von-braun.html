Three full-height photographs in matched frames, captions sitting on the pictures. The portrait stands alone first, so the room can be asked who he is before anything is claimed. Then the Saturn V, the answer they expect; then the V-2, the one they do not — the same man, the same launch frame, and only where it came down is different. Last, all three blur out and the Lehrer lyric drops over them.

“Once the rockets are up, who cares where they come down? That’s not my department!”

— Tom Lehrer, “Wernher von Braun,” 1965

Hold on step 0 and actually ask the room — most will not place the face, a few will. Give them the Saturn V first; that is the version of him NASA put on television. Then the V-2: fired at London and Antwerp, and say plainly that the labour was slave labour — more people died building it than were killed by it. The Lehrer line is satire, not a quotation from von Braun, so say so. Then stop and let the last question sit; do not answer it. — Image credits (all public domain, no attribution required): portrait, NASA / MSFC, Fred Deaton, May 1964.
