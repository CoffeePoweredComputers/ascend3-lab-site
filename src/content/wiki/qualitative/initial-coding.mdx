**First-cycle coding** is your first systematic pass: break the data into chunks and
attach a short label to each <Cite id="saldana-2021" />. Two workhorse styles:

- **Descriptive coding** — *you* summarize the chunk in a word or phrase (`help-seeking delay`).
- **In vivo coding** — the code *is the participant's own words* (`"think I was dumb"`),
  preserving their voice <Cite id="charmaz-2014" />.

This stage is shared by both traditions — everyone codes the data first. What differs
(next lessons) is what you do *with* the codes.

<Callout type="note" title="One chunk can get several codes">
Segments often carry more than one code, and the same code recurs across participants —
that recurrence is the raw material of categories. Code generously now; you'll prune later
<Cite id="saldana-2021" />.

## Inductive, deductive, abductive?

Where do codes come from? Three stances, and most analysts move between them:

| Reasoning | How codes arise | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inductive** | codes emerge from the data | <Cite id="thomas-2006" /> |
| **Deductive** | a pre-set framework is applied first | <Cite id="hsieh-shannon-2005" /> |
| **Abductive** | looping between data and theory — where most analysts land, guided by "sensitizing concepts" | <Cite id="timmermans-tavory-2012" />, <Cite id="blumer-1954" /> |
