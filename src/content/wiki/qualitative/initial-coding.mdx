**First-cycle coding** is your first systematic pass: break the data into chunks and
attach a short label to each <Cite id="saldana-2021" />. Two workhorse **semantic** styles —
codes that stay close to what was said:

- **Descriptive coding** — *you* summarize the chunk in a word or phrase (`help-seeking delay`).
- **In vivo coding** — the code *is the participant's own words* (`"think I was dumb"`),
  preserving their voice <Cite id="charmaz-2014" />.

The *mechanic* — segment, then label — is common to both traditions. But the **coding stance
already diverges here**, and neither is the neutral default:

- The **codebook / coding-reliability** path favors **semantic** codes like the ones above:
  close to the surface, so they can be defined and applied consistently across coders. They
  feed the codebook in the next lessons.
- **Reflexive TA** leans on **latent** coding — reading for the meaning *underneath* the
  words. The [Reflexive Coding](/wiki/qualitative/reflexive-coding-and-themes) lesson recodes
  these *same excerpts* that way (e.g. `"think I was dumb"` → `managing how I am seen`).

The worked example below uses the semantic stance — read it as *one* reading, not the default.

<Callout type="note" title="Codes (and themes) cut across participants">
Segments often carry more than one code, and the same code recurs across participants —
that recurrence is the raw material of categories. Notice the spread above: each student's
excerpt touches **several** themes (P3 is both a help-seeking *and* a debugging story), and
each theme is built from **several** students (improvised debugging shows up in P3, P7, and
P9). A theme is a pattern *across the dataset*, not a one-line summary of one person. Code
generously now; you'll prune later <Cite id="saldana-2021" />.
</Callout>

## Inductive, deductive, abductive?

Where do codes come from? Three stances, and most analysts move between them:

| Reasoning | How codes arise | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inductive** | codes emerge from the data | <Cite id="thomas-2006" /> |
| **Deductive** | a pre-set framework is applied first | <Cite id="hsieh-shannon-2005" /> |
| **Abductive** | looping between data and theory — where most analysts land, guided by "sensitizing concepts" | <Cite id="timmermans-tavory-2012" />, <Cite id="blumer-1954" /> |
