Codes are not themes. A **theme** captures a *patterned meaning* organized around a central
idea <Cite id="braun-clarke-2022" />. Building them is the affinity move: cluster related
codes, then ask "what's the story these tell together?"

<Steps>
1. **Generate candidate themes.** Cluster codes that share meaning (an affinity-diagram move).
2. **Review against the data.** Does each theme hold across the extracts? Split, merge, or drop.
3. **Define and name.** Write a sentence or two stating each theme's *central organizing concept*.
4. **Select compelling extracts** that evidence the theme, and weave them into the write-up.
</Steps>

<Callout type="tip" title="The lesson of the whole module">
Method is a choice with consequences. The codebook path buys you transferability and a
reliability claim; the reflexive path buys you interpretive depth. Choose the one your
question and epistemology call for — and report it honestly <Cite id="braun-clarke-2021-onesize" />
