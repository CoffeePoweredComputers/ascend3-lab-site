Coding too early is the classic beginner mistake. Both traditions start the same way:
**immerse yourself in the data**, then **write memos** — informal analytic notes that
capture hunches before they harden into codes <Cite id="saldana-2021" /> <Cite id="charmaz-2014" />.

<Steps>
1. **Read the whole transcript** once without labeling anything. Get the gist.
2. **Re-read and jot.** In the margins, note what strikes you — surprises, repetitions, contradictions, emotion.
3. **Write an analytic memo.** A few sentences: what might be going on here? What questions does it raise?
4. **Hold your jottings loosely.** They are candidates for codes, not codes yet.
</Steps>

<Callout type="tip" title="Memos are data too">
Keep memos in a running document with dates. They become your audit trail
<Cite id="lincoln-guba-1985" /> and, in reflexive work, a record of how your reading
evolved <Cite id="braun-clarke-2022" />.
</Callout>
