Quantitative analysis is about answering questions with numbers — measuring effects,
estimating uncertainty, and reporting results others can reproduce. This module runs
from descriptive statistics through regression, multilevel models, and SEM, then goes
deep on **measurement**: how to build, judge, and reuse the assessments that produce
your numbers in the first place.

## The running example

Two artifacts travel through every lesson, and both ship as real files you can analyze:

- **A debugging-intervention study** ([debugging-study.csv](/templates/debugging-study.csv)) —
  the lab pilots *SPRINT*, a systematic-debugging intervention, with students attempting
  12 debugging exercises: pre/post test scores, per-attempt fixed/not-fixed outcomes,
  time-to-fix, and a short self-efficacy scale, across two course sections.
- **A Debugging Concept Inventory pilot** ([dci-pilot.csv](/templates/dci-pilot.csv)) —
  150 students' responses to a draft 20-item debugging test. The measurement lessons
  build this instrument's evidence step by step — including finding the two items that
  are deliberately broken.

Both datasets are synthetic (generated by `scripts/generate-quant-data.mjs`), so every
code snippet in the module runs against them with no IRB concerns. They continue the
wiki's running thread: the [qualitative module](/wiki/qualitative/overview) coded
interviews about debugging; here, those experiences become numbers.

## A typical workflow

- Define variables and how each is measured.
- Explore with descriptive statistics and plots.
- Choose a model that matches your design and data — *design first, test names later*.
- Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not just p-values.

<Callout type="warning" title="Plan analysis before collecting data">
Deciding your analysis after seeing the data invites p-hacking. Pre-register or at
least write your analysis plan first.
</Callout>

Whatever it suggests, report an **effect size** and a confidence interval, not just a
p-value — the [effect sizes lesson](/wiki/quantitative/effect-sizes-and-reporting) is
where that habit gets built.

## What this module deliberately skips

Bayesian methods, learning analytics / educational data mining, knowledge tracing,
meta-analysis, survival analysis, computerized adaptive testing, and multidimensional
IRT are real and useful — and out of scope. Each gets a pointer where relevant
(e.g., EDM's canonical review is <a href="/wiki/quantitative/references#ref-ihantola-etal-2015">Ihantola et al. 2015</a>).
