Before any test, describe your data: central tendency, spread, and shape.

- **Center** — mean, median, mode.
- **Spread** — standard deviation, IQR, range.
- **Shape** — skew, outliers, modality (look at a histogram!).

## Shape changes everything

Time-to-fix data is the classic case. In
[debugging-study.csv](/templates/debugging-study.csv), minutes-to-fix has a mean of
about 9.2 but a median of 6.7 — most fixes are quick, a long tail of stuck students
drags the mean up, and a 30-minute timeout piles censored attempts at the cap. Switch
the shape below and watch the **mean** (solid) chase the tail while the **median**
(dashed) barely moves:

That gap is why you always plot your data before trusting a single summary number.

## Spread: how much do values vary?

A center is only half the story — you also need to report how spread out the data is, and
which spread measure you pick should match your center:

| Measure | What it captures | Pairs with | Sensitive to outliers? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard deviation** | typical distance of values from the mean | mean | Yes |
| **IQR** (interquartile range) | the span of the middle 50% | median | No |
| **Range** | min to max | — | Very |

Rule of thumb: report **mean ± SD** for roughly symmetric data, and **median (IQR)** when the
distribution is skewed or has outliers — the same logic as choosing the center. For the
running example's minutes: *median 6.7 (IQR …)*, not *mean 9.2 ± …*.

<Reveal summary="When should I prefer the median over the mean?">
When the distribution is skewed or has outliers. The mean is pulled toward extreme
values; the median is robust to them, so it better represents a "typical" case.
Time-on-task data is almost always in this category.
</Reveal>

## One more habit: know your unit

`debugging-study.csv` has 462 rows but only 40 students — student-level columns
(pre, post, self-efficacy) repeat on every attempt row. Summarize *students* on a
one-row-per-student version, and *attempts* on the full table. Mixing the two silently
weights students by how many exercises they attempted — a first taste of the
independence problems the [clustered-data lesson](/wiki/quantitative/clustered-data-mlm-gee)
takes seriously.
