Before any test, describe your data: central tendency, spread, and shape.

- **Center** — mean, median, mode.
- **Spread** — standard deviation, IQR, range.
- **Shape** — skew, outliers, modality (look at a histogram!).

## Shape changes everything

The mean and median agree only when the data is symmetric. Switch the shape below and
watch the **mean** (solid) chase the tail while the **median** (dashed) barely moves:

<DistributionPlot />

That gap is why you always plot your data before trusting a single summary number.

## Spread: how much do values vary?

A center is only half the story — you also need to report how spread out the data is, and
which spread measure you pick should match your center:

| Measure | What it captures | Pairs with | Sensitive to outliers? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard deviation** | typical distance of values from the mean | mean | Yes |
| **IQR** (interquartile range) | the span of the middle 50% | median | No |
| **Range** | min to max | — | Very |

Rule of thumb: report **mean ± SD** for roughly symmetric data, and **median (IQR)** when the
distribution is skewed or has outliers — the same logic as choosing the center.

<Reveal summary="When should I prefer the median over the mean?">
When the distribution is skewed or has outliers. The mean is pulled toward extreme
values; the median is robust to them, so it better represents a "typical" case.
</Reveal>
