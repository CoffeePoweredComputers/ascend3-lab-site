## Three or more groups: ANOVA

Comparing three course formats with three t-tests triples your false-alarm rate. A
**one-way ANOVA** asks one question first — *do the group means differ at all?* — then
**post-hoc tests** (Tukey's HSD handles the correction) tell you where. The rank-based
analogue is **Kruskal–Wallis**.

**Factorial** designs cross factors (format × prior experience), and the payoff is the
**interaction**: does the format effect *depend on* experience? When an interaction is
present, report simple effects (the format effect at each experience level), not the
misleading main effects. Report η² or partial η² per
[the checklist](/wiki/quantitative/effect-sizes-and-reporting) <Cite id="lakens-2013" />.

**ANCOVA** — ANOVA with a covariate, most often the pre-test — is the precision move
for pre/post designs, and (as the walkthrough below shows) it is really just
[regression](/wiki/quantitative/linear-regression) wearing a costume.

<CodeTabs>
  <Fragment slot="r">

```r
students <- dplyr::distinct(read.csv("debugging-study.csv"),
                            student, section, pre, post)
fit <- aov(post ~ pre + section, data = students)   # ANCOVA
summary(fit)
effectsize::eta_squared(fit, partial = TRUE)
```

```python
import pandas as pd, pingouin as pg

students = (pd.read_csv("debugging-study.csv")
              [["student", "section", "pre", "post"]].drop_duplicates())
pg.ancova(data=students, dv="post", covar="pre", between="section")
```

## Categorical outcomes: chi-square and Fisher

When the outcome is a category — fixed the bug / didn't — counts go in a table and the
**chi-square test of independence** asks whether the proportions differ. Two cautions:

- **Small cells** (expected counts under ~5, routine in class-sized data): use
  **Fisher's exact test**.
- **Effect size**: report the proportions themselves and their difference (or an odds
  ratio) — a bare χ² and p says nothing about magnitude.

Chi-square is one of CER's "big three" <Cite id="sanders-etal-2019" />, and its most
common misuse in the wild is running it on *attempt-level* counts where the same
student contributes many rows — the independence problem again, handled properly in
[clustered data](/wiki/quantitative/clustered-data-mlm-gee).

<Callout type="note" title="Where's the model version?">
Chi-square on a 2×2 is the test-shaped view; [logistic regression](/wiki/quantitative/logistic-regression)
is the model-shaped view of the same question, with covariates and CIs on an
interpretable scale. Same fork as ANOVA vs regression.
</Callout>
