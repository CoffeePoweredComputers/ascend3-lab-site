## Three words that are not synonyms

- **Impact** — groups differ in average score. Not evidence of unfairness: real
  ability differences (prior CS exposure, say) produce impact on a perfectly fair test.
- **DIF (differential item functioning)** — students *matched on ability* succeed on
  an item at different rates by group. The item behaves differently conditional on θ.
- **Bias** — DIF *plus a substantive judgment* that the difference is
  construct-irrelevant (culture-bound context, language load). Flagged DIF is a
  hypothesis about bias, never a verdict.

Confusing these produces both false alarms ("groups scored differently — the test is
biased!") and false comfort ("no mean difference — the test is fair!"). Both
inferences are wrong; item-level analysis conditional on ability is the tool.

## The standard detection methods

| Method | Detects | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mantel–Haenszel** | uniform | matches on total score; the classic, with ETS's A/B/C effect-size bands |
| **Logistic regression** | uniform + non-uniform | add group and group×score terms; effect-size gate on ΔR² |
| **IRT-based** (likelihood ratio, Lord) | both | compares item parameters across groups; wants the largest samples |

All share one loop: match on ability, test each item, **purify** (re-match excluding
flagged items, re-test), then submit flags to *human judgment* about content. Working
guidance: ~200 per group before flags stabilize — the walkthrough's arithmetic.

## Real findings to calibrate on

- An SCS1-derived exam carried three gender-DIF items — inside an otherwise
  acceptable total score <Cite id="davidson-etal-2021" />. Item-level bias hides.
- Bias can live *below* the item: differential distractor functioning — groups
  matched on ability drawn to different wrong answers.
- Fairness-first is possible: newer instruments run DIF during development, not as a
  post-hoc audit — the direction of travel for the DCI plan.

<Reveal summary="Check: the DCI shows impact by prior-experience group but no DIF. What can you claim?">
That the *items* function equivalently across groups at matched ability — the score
difference reflects whatever ability difference exists, as far as this evidence goes.
You cannot claim the test is "fair" full stop (DIF is one fairness lens, on one
grouping, with this sample's power), and you cannot claim the experience gap is
innate — impact says nothing about *why* groups differ. Two careful sentences beat
one sweeping one.
</Reveal>

<Callout type="note" title="The SEM cousin">
For multi-item *scales*, the same question — does the instrument measure the same
construct across groups? — is asked as **measurement invariance** via multi-group CFA
(see the [SEM lesson](/wiki/quantitative/sem)). DIF is the item-level view, invariance
the factor-level view, of one concern.
</Callout>
