The model is chosen; what does calibration actually buy you? Three things CTT can't
deliver: precision that varies by ability, machinery for checking items individually,
and item parameters that (approximately) survive changes of sample.

## Information: where your test is precise

Each item carries **information** concentrated near its difficulty; steeper items
carry more of it. Toggle the dashed information curve and watch it track the slope:

<IccExplorer showInfo />

Summing item information gives the **test information function**, and the payoff line
of the whole framework: **conditional SEM = 1/√information**. A test is only precise
*where its information is* — the DCI, with difficulties centered near average θ,
measures middling students well and says little about the extremes. (The published
version of this exact finding: the SCS1 measures best at mid-ability and poorly at
the low end its users often care about <Cite id="xie-etal-2019" />.) Choosing items to
*place* information is how instrument revision becomes engineering instead of taste.

## Scoring

Once items are calibrated, a student's θ estimate comes from maximum likelihood
(undefined for all-right/all-wrong response strings) or the Bayesian **EAP/MAP**
estimators (defined everywhere, shrunk slightly toward the mean) — one line each is
all you need until you build a CAT. Report θ with its *conditional* SE, not a single
test-wide band.

## The assumptions that earn the fit

1. **Unidimensionality** — one dominant construct; checked with the factor tools from
   [reliability screening](/wiki/quantitative/reliability-and-item-analysis), not assumed.
2. **Local independence** — after accounting for θ, items are independent. The
   CER-native violation: several items hanging off one shared code snippet (a
   testlet). Check residual correlations (Q3); model or merge offenders.
3. **Monotonicity** — P(correct) rises with θ; the pilot's mis-keyed i07 fails this
   spectacularly, which is why screening caught it first.

Item-level **fit statistics** (S-X², infit/outfit in the Rasch tradition) flag items
the model can't explain — treat flags as *revision prompts*, not automatic deletions
<Cite id="planinic-etal-2019" />.

## Polytomous models: the survey side

Likert items are ordered categories, and IRT handles them natively — no
means-on-ordinal compromise. The **Graded Response Model** gives each item a set of
threshold curves (the running example: the six-item debugging self-efficacy scale);
the **Partial Credit Model** does the same for rubric scores. Same assumptions, same
information logic, GRM-scale sample-size demands (~500 for comfort)
<Cite id="deayala-2022" />.

```r
library(mirt)
dci <- read.csv("dci-pilot.csv")[, -1]
rasch <- mirt(dci, 1, itemtype = "Rasch")
coef(rasch, simplify = TRUE, IRTpars = TRUE)   # b per item, with SEs
itemfit(rasch)                                 # S-X2 flags
plot(rasch, type = "info")                     # test information function
```

<Callout type="note" title="Equating, in one paragraph">
Pre/post with two test forms is secretly an **equating** problem: raw scores from
different forms aren't comparable until linked (common anchor items, calibrated on a
shared scale). Full treatment: Kolen & Brennan <Cite id="kolen-brennan-2014" />. If
you take one thing: reusing 5–6 anchor items across forms is what makes later linking
*possible* — design it in now. Computerized adaptive testing is the same machinery
pointed at item selection; know the term, skip the details.
</Callout>
