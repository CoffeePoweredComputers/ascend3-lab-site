Classical test theory has three quiet limitations: item statistics depend on *which
students* you piloted on, person scores depend on *which items* you happened to ask,
and one SEM pretends the test is equally precise for everyone. **Item response
theory** fixes all three by modeling the *encounter between a person and an item*
<Cite id="embretson-reise-2000" />.

## The item characteristic curve

One item, one curve: the probability of answering correctly as a function of ability
**θ**. Drag the parameters:

<IccExplorer />

- **b (difficulty)** slides the curve left–right — *where on the ability scale the
  item works*. Persons and items share the θ scale: "this item's b is 1.2" and "this
  student's θ is 1.2" are locations on the same ruler.
- **a (discrimination)** sets the steepness — how sharply the item separates ability
  near b.
- **c (lower asymptote)** lifts the left tail — the "guessing" parameter, and *not*
  simply 1/(number of options): it's estimated, badly, unless N is large.

Two habits to install immediately: **θ is not percent-correct** (its zero is a
convention — the scale's location and spread are arbitrary), and each curve is a
*model claim* to be checked against data, not a fact.

## The model ladder

| Model | Free per item | Costs (respondents) | Buys you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rasch / 1PL** | b | ~100–250 <Cite id="linacre-1994" /> | common scale, raw score sufficiency, strict item-quality lens |
| **2PL** | a, b | ~500 <Cite id="deayala-2022" /> | items weighted by discrimination |
| **3PL** | a, b, c | ~1000+ | guessing floor — when you can afford it |

**The Rasch-vs-IRT divide** is philosophical, not algebraic: the Rasch tradition
treats the model as a *definition of measurement* — items that misfit are flawed and
get fixed or dropped; the IRT tradition treats models as *descriptions* — if items
vary in discrimination, estimate it. You will meet both camps in the literature;
know that 1PL-vs-2PL fights are often this worldview fight in disguise
<Cite id="boone-2016" />.

<Callout type="warning" title="The lab-scale punchline">
Course-sized samples (N &lt; 200) support **CTT or Rasch — not 2PL, not 3PL**. Fitting
an under-resourced model produces parameters that won't replicate, and instrument
revisions chasing them churn real damage. This single warning prevents more bad
psychometrics than any formula in this block.
</Callout>
