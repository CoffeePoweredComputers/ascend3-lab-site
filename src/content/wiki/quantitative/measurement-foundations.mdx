Every number in this module so far came from an instrument — a test, a survey, a log.
Measurement theory asks the prior question: *do those numbers mean what we need them
to mean?* For a lab that does assessment work, this block is the center of gravity.

## Constructs and latent variables

"Debugging ability" cannot be observed. It's a **construct** — a theoretical variable
we reach only through observable proxies: answers to items, time on task, self-report.
A score is therefore an **inference**, and the whole game is keeping that inference
defensible. Two ways it fails:

- **Construct underrepresentation** — the instrument misses part of the construct
  (a debugging test that's all *reading* and no *actually locating* bugs).
- **Construct-irrelevant variance** — scores move for the wrong reasons (a "debugging"
  item that mostly measures obscure language trivia, or test anxiety).

**Levels of measurement** (nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio) tell you which
arithmetic your scores support — the issue behind the
[Likert discussion](/wiki/quantitative/glms-beyond-binary), now with its proper roots.

## The true-score model

Classical test theory (CTT) starts from one equation:

> **X = T + E** — observed score = true score + error.

From this modest model comes everything in the [next lesson](/wiki/quantitative/reliability-and-item-analysis):
reliability is the share of observed-score variance that is true-score variance, and
the **standard error of measurement** puts a ± band around every individual score.
Note one built-in limitation the IRT lessons will fix: CTT's SEM is one number *for
everyone*, as if the test were equally precise for your strongest and weakest
students <Cite id="bandalos-2018" />.

## "Validated" is not a property

Say it precisely: **validity is a property of score *interpretations* for specific
*uses* in specific *populations*** — not of instruments <Cite id="aera-apa-ncme-2014" />.
"The SCS1 is validated" is the canonical wrong sentence; what exists is *validity
evidence* for *certain uses* — and the [instruments lesson](/wiki/quantitative/instruments-in-practice)
shows how that evidence eroded as people modified the SCS1. Anyone shortening,
translating, or repurposing an instrument is starting a new validity argument, not
inheriting the old one.

<Reveal summary="Check yourself: 'we used a validated debugging self-efficacy scale, so our measures are solid.' What's wrong?">
Three things. Validated *for whom* — the original population may not resemble intro-CS
students. Validated *for what use* — a research instrument's evidence may not support
individual decisions. And "validated" is not a state: it's an argument whose strength
you must actually inspect (what evidence exists, of which kinds?). The sentence
outsources judgment to a past-tense verb.
</Reveal>
