Classroom data is nested: attempts inside students, students inside sections, sections
inside semesters. Nested observations share things — a student's skill colors all 12
of their attempts — so they are **not independent**, and every SE in this module so far
assumed they were. Ignore that (**pseudoreplication**) and your CIs are too narrow,
your p-values too confident <Cite id="theobald-2018" />.

**How much does nesting matter?** The **intraclass correlation** — here, the share of
outcome variance that lives *between* students — answers it. (Same "ICC" acronym as
the agreement statistic in the [IRR lesson](/wiki/qualitative/inter-rater-reliability),
and different again from the item characteristic curve coming in
[IRT](/wiki/quantitative/irt-fundamentals). Three meanings, one abbreviation —
context tells you which.)

## Two fixes, two different claims

- **Mixed-effects (multilevel) models** give each student their own intercept (and
  maybe slope). Estimates are **conditional** — *for a given student* — and the model
  extends naturally (crossed effects for exercises, students within sections)
  <Cite id="gelman-hill-vehtari-2020" />.
- **GEE** models the population average directly, with a working guess at the
  within-student correlation and **robust (sandwich) SEs** that forgive a wrong guess.
  Estimates are **marginal** — *across the student population*.

For linear models the two roughly coincide. For **logistic** they genuinely don't:
marginal ORs sit closer to 1 than conditional ORs, so *which model you fit changes the
number you report* <Cite id="hubbard-etal-2010" />. Neither is "more correct" — they
answer different questions, and cluster-robust SEs on a simple model are often a third
honest option <Cite id="mcneish-etal-2017" />.

```r
library(lme4); library(geepack)
attempts <- read.csv("debugging-study.csv")

# Conditional: mixed-effects logistic (random intercept per student)
mm <- glmer(fixed ~ section + exercise + (1 | student),
            data = attempts, family = binomial)

# Marginal: GEE with exchangeable working correlation + robust SEs
gee <- geeglm(fixed ~ section + exercise, id = factor(student),
              data = attempts, family = binomial, corstr = "exchangeable")
summary(gee)   # 40 clusters — small-sample-correct the SEs (Mancl–DeRouen)
```

```python
import pandas as pd, statsmodels.formula.api as smf
import statsmodels.api as sm

attempts = pd.read_csv("debugging-study.csv")

mm = smf.mixedlm("fixed ~ C(section) + C(exercise)", attempts,
                 groups=attempts.student).fit()          # linear approx.

gee = smf.gee("fixed ~ C(section) + C(exercise)", "student", attempts,
              family=sm.families.Binomial(),
              cov_struct=sm.cov_struct.Exchangeable()).fit()
print(gee.summary())
```

## Honesty beats

- **GEE wants clusters** — sandwich SEs behave from roughly 30–40 clusters up; correct
  them below ~50 <Cite id="mancl-derouen-2001" />.
- **Two sections is not a multilevel design.** With 2 clusters, "section" is a fixed
  effect and nesting is a *limitation to write about*, not a model term
  <Cite id="mcneish-stapleton-2016" />.
- **Missingness differs**: mixed models tolerate MAR skipping; standard GEE leans on
  MCAR. Students skipping harder exercises tilts the field toward the mixed model as
  at least a sensitivity analysis.
