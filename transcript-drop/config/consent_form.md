# Consent to Take Part in a Research Study

**Title of research study:** A Longitudinal Resource of Naturally Occurring
Student–GenAI Interactions in Collaborative Software Development (Virginia Tech
IRB # 26-883)

**Principal Investigator:** David H. Smith IV, Ph.D., Department of Computer
Science, +1-360-969-3836, dhsmith4@vt.edu

**Other study contact:** Minsun Kim, minsunkim@vt.edu

## Key information

You are being asked to take part in a research study. Taking part is voluntary,
and **it has no effect on your grade** in CS 2114, on your standing in the
course, or on your access to course resources. The study does not collect your
grades.

The study collects conversations you have already had with GenAI tools while
working on your CS 2114 projects, and links them to the development history in
your team's GitHub repository. **You choose which conversations, if any, to
contribute, every time.** Nothing is monitored or captured automatically, and
nothing is installed on your computer that watches what you do.

Taking part costs about 5 minutes now, and about 5–10 minutes at each of the
four designated group-work sessions across the semester.

The main risk is to your privacy, because the conversations you contribute may
contain personal information. What we do about that is described below. There
are no direct benefits to you, though contributing to each of the three projects
makes you eligible for a prize drawing.

## Detailed information

### Who can take part?

Virginia Tech students who are **18 years of age or older** and enrolled in Fall
2026 CS 2114. Up to about 350 students may be invited. You are asked to confirm
your age before you read this form; if you are under 18 you cannot take part in
this study.

### What happens if I say yes?

Four designated group-work sessions take place across the three course projects.
At each one you may choose whether to contribute one or more GenAI conversations
connected to that project. You decide each time — agreeing now does not oblige
you to contribute at any particular session, or at all, and you may skip any
session without penalty.

You can contribute a conversation in whichever way suits the tool you used:

- upload the conversation file the tool exports,
- paste a shareable link to the conversation, or
- copy and paste the conversation text.

For each conversation you contribute, you answer one short question about what
kind of programming work it was.

You may also give us the address of your team's GitHub repository. **We will only
use a repository if every member of that team has consented.** If even one
teammate has not consented, that repository is excluded from the study and any
copy we made is deleted.

We never ask for a password, an API key, or any other credential.

### What happens if I say yes, but change my mind later?

You can leave the study at any time, for any reason, and it will not be held
against you.

Through the end of the Fall 2026 semester you can also withdraw what you have
already contributed, by contacting Minsun Kim at minsunkim@vt.edu or David H.
Smith IV at dhsmith4@vt.edu. After the semester ends — once withdrawal requests
are resolved and the prize drawing is complete — the link between you and your
contributions is destroyed as part of de-identification. From that point your
records can no longer be found, so they cannot be withdrawn.

### Is there any way being in this study could be bad for me?

This study is expected to involve no more than minimal risk. The risk is to your
privacy, and three things carry it.

- **The conversations themselves.** We store what you send us as written, and we
  cannot know in advance what is in it. A conversation might contain names, email
  addresses, account usernames, repository addresses, exact times, source code,
  references to your teammates, or something personal you happened to type. You
  see the full text of every conversation before you send it, and you choose
  which ones to include.
- **Your team's repository history.** Git records author names, email addresses
  and account usernames in every commit. We treat these as identifiers and remove
  or replace them when the data is de-identified.
- **Reviewing your own conversations.** Some people find it uncomfortable to go
  back through what they wrote, particularly if a conversation turns out to
  contain something they would rather keep private. You can simply not include it.

A confidentiality breach could cause limited social, academic or reputational
harm. We reduce that risk by keeping your email address in a separate file from
your conversations, which are stored under a study code instead. Study data is
held on Virginia Tech-managed storage, access is limited to study personnel
listed on the IRB protocol and granted on a least-privilege basis, and the link
between you and your code is destroyed after the semester.

Because this study is run inside a course you are taking, you might feel some
pressure to take part. Participation is voluntary, the course instructor is not
present during recruitment, your grades are not collected, and nothing about your
participation is used in any grading decision.

We cannot promise complete confidentiality. The Virginia Tech IRB and other
organizations responsible for oversight of research may inspect and copy study
records.

There are no physical, legal, economic or medical risks to taking part, and no
cost to you.

### Are there any benefits to me?

There are no direct benefits to you. Taking part may prompt you to think about
how you use GenAI while programming, but that is not guaranteed and is not a
benefit of the study. The value of the study is the research resource created
from what participants contribute.

### Will I be paid?

Participants who make at least one contribution for **each** of the three course
projects become eligible for one end-of-study prize drawing. Contributing more
than that does not add entries, and eligibility does not depend on your grades,
or on what your conversations contain.

Up to 34 participants are drawn at random, and nobody receives more than one
prize:

- one $100 Amazon gift card,
- three $30 Starbucks gift cards,
- thirty $10 Starbucks gift cards.

If at least 34 people are eligible, your overall chance of a prize is 34 divided
by the number of eligible participants; for the $100 prize it is 1 divided by
that number, for a $30 prize 3 divided by it, and for a $10 prize 30 divided by
it. Winners are removed before the next tier is drawn. If fewer than 34 people
are eligible, the remaining prizes are not redistributed.

The drawing happens after the final donation period and before the link between
you and your records is destroyed, because we need that link to contact you.

### What happens to the information collected?

The portal records that you consented, that you confirmed your eligibility, your
study code, your VT email address, your project and team, the time of each
submission, the conversations you chose to contribute, your answer to the
interaction-type question, and your team's repository address if you give it. We
do not collect your name.

Your conversations are stored under a study code, separately from your email
address, for the length of the semester. After the semester that link is
destroyed, leaving a de-identified corpus.

Results may be presented in summary form at conferences, in presentations, in
academic papers, and as part of a thesis or dissertation, and the de-identified
research data may be released publicly. If identifiers are removed from the
information collected during this research, that information could be used for
future research studies, or shared with another investigator for future research,
without your additional consent.

### Can I be removed from the study without my OK?

No. The research team will not remove you from the study. You may leave it
yourself at any time.

If you have questions, concerns or complaints, or think the research has hurt

dhsmith4@vt.edu, or Minsun Kim at minsunkim@vt.edu.

This research has been reviewed and approved by the Virginia Tech Institutional
Review Board (IRB). You may contact them at 540-231-3732 or irb@vt.edu if:

- you have questions about your rights as a research subject,
- your questions, concerns or complaints are not being answered by the research

- you cannot reach the research team, or
- you want to talk to someone besides the research team about this research.
